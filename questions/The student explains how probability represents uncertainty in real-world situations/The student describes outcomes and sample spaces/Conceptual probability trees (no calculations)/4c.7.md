##### Explanation
A probability tree's endpoints represent the complete set of distinct outcome pathways. Because only patients with elevated BP proceed to the cholesterol stage, the tree has three endpoints (not two or four). The path ending at normal BP is one complete outcome, while the elevated BP path branches into two additional outcomes at the cholesterol stage. Correctly listing all three paths demonstrates understanding of how sequential branching in a probability tree defines a sample space.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Normal BP; Elevated BP — Lists only the first-stage results and treats them as the complete sample space. This targets students who stop at the initial branch and do not trace the tree to its endpoints, failing to recognize that the second-stage branching creates additional distinct outcomes.

###### Option 2
Elevated BP with cholesterol within range; Elevated BP with cholesterol out of range — Includes only the outcomes from the second stage, omitting patients whose pathway ends at normal BP. This targets students who focus on the deeper branches of the tree while overlooking that a pathway can terminate at an earlier stage and still count as a complete outcome.

###### Option 3
Normal BP with cholesterol within range; Normal BP with cholesterol out of range; Elevated BP with cholesterol within range; Elevated BP with cholesterol out of range — Incorrectly assumes every patient passes through both stages, producing four outcomes instead of three. This targets students who do not recognize the conditional nature of the branching, treating the tree as though all first-stage results lead to the same second-stage split.
