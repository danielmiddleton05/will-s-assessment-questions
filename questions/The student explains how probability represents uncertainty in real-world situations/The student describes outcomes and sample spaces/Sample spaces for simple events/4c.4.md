#### Task Description

**Given:** A diagnostic test for a specific condition reports one of three results for each patient: Positive, Negative, or Inconclusive. A medical records clerk creates a summary chart to track test results. The clerk records outcomes only as Positive or Negative, leaving out any Inconclusive results from the chart.

How does the clerk's recording method affect the representation of the sample space?

###### Option 2 Correct
It produces an incomplete sample space by excluding a defined outcome.


###### Option 3 Incorrect

###### Option 4 Incorrect

##### Explanation
A sample space must include all possible outcomes of an event as defined by the scenario. The diagnostic test is explicitly defined as producing three possible results: Positive, Negative, or Inconclusive. By omitting Inconclusive, the clerk's chart no longer represents the full sample space. A valid sample space must be exhaustive—it must account for every outcome that can occur. Regardless of whether an outcome seems ambiguous or less definitive, if it is a defined possible result of the event, it must be included in the sample space. Therefore, the clerk's method produces an incomplete sample space.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
"It has no effect because Inconclusive is not a definitive outcome." — This targets students who mistakenly believe that only clear-cut or decisive outcomes belong in a sample space. Students may reason that because Inconclusive does not confirm or deny the condition, it is not a "real" result. However, the scenario explicitly defines Inconclusive as one of the three possible test results, so it is a valid outcome that must be included.

###### Option 3
"It improves the sample space by eliminating an ambiguous category." — This targets students who confuse simplification with correctness. A student may think that removing a less clear-cut category makes the sample space better or cleaner. In reality, removing a defined possible outcome violates the requirement that a sample space be exhaustive, regardless of whether the outcome seems ambiguous.

###### Option 4
"It produces a more precise sample space by narrowing the possible outcomes." — This targets students who associate fewer outcomes with greater precision or accuracy. A student may believe that a smaller set of outcomes is inherently more focused and therefore better. However, precision in a sample space comes from correctly including all and only the defined possible outcomes, not from reducing their number.
