##### Explanation
To identify which outcome is least likely, we must calculate and compare the probability of each event:

1.  **Coin Flip:** A fair coin has two possible outcomes. The probability of landing on heads is 1 out of 2, which is $1/2$ or $0.50$.
2.  **Die Roll:** A fair six-sided die has six possible outcomes. The probability of rolling a "1" or a "2" is 2 out of 6. This simplifies to $1/3$, which is approximately $0.33$.

Comparing the two results: $0.33 < 0.50$. Since the probability of the die roll ($1/3$) is less than the probability of the coin flip ($1/2$), rolling a "1" or a "2" is the least likely outcome.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The coin landing on heads — This may be selected if the student confuses the terms "least likely" and "most likely," or if they incorrectly believe that $1/2$ is a smaller value than $1/3$.

###### Option 3
Both outcomes are equally likely — This may be selected if the student miscalculates the fractions or incorrectly assumes that any "simple" random event has the same probability regardless of the number of sides or outcomes.

###### Option 4
The probabilities cannot be compared because a coin and a die have different numbers of sides — This may be selected if the student believes that probability comparison is only possible when the sample spaces (the total number of possible outcomes) are identical.
