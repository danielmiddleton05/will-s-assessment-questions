#### Task Description

**Given:** A cybersecurity tool's testing report states that the probability of successfully detecting a specific known virus in a scanned file is 1.

What real-world conclusion about virus detection can be drawn from this probability?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The tool will fail to detect the known virus during a scan.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The tool is unlikely to miss the known virus during a scan.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The tool usually detects the known virus, but may occasionally miss one.


###### Option 4 Correct
The tool will detect the known virus every time a file is scanned.

##### Competency

##### Explanation
A probability of 1 means the event is certain—it will occur every time without exception. Because the report states the probability of successful detection is 1, the real-world meaning is that the tool will detect a known virus in every scanned file, every time. This question requires the student to interpret a numerical probability value and translate it into a concrete real-world conclusion, rather than simply labeling a described scenario with a vocabulary term.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
"The tool will fail to detect the known virus during a scan." — Confuses opposite ends of the probability scale. This targets students who mix up a probability of 1 (certain to happen) with a probability of 0 (impossible). A student selecting this option reverses the meaning of the two extreme probability values.

###### Option 2
"The tool is unlikely to miss the known virus during a scan" — Substitutes "unlikely to fail" for "certain to succeed." This targets students who recognize the positive direction of the probability but frame the conclusion using "unlikely," which implies there is still some small chance of failure. A probability of 1 leaves no room for failure, so "unlikely to miss" understates the certainty.

###### Option 3
"The tool usually detects the known virus, but may occasionally miss one" — Confuses high probability with certainty. This targets students who interpret a probability of 1 as meaning "very high but not perfect," treating it as a likely event rather than a certain one. The phrase "may occasionally miss one" contradicts the absolute certainty indicated by a probability of 1.
