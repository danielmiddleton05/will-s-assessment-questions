**Given:** A small business owner is projecting monthly cash flow and considers two events: "The monthly office rent will be due on the 1st" and "A new customer will walk into the store in the next hour."

How do these two events differ in terms of probability?

###### Option 2 Incorrect

##### Evidence Statement

##### Aligned Content
Understanding chance vs certainty

##### Explanation
A fixed contractual obligation like rent is considered essentially certain (probability of 1) within the business model, while a customer arrival involves genuine uncertainty—it may or may not happen. This distinction between certainty and chance is fundamental to understanding when probability is meaningful for modeling outcomes.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
The customer arrival is certain while the rent due date involves chance — Reverses the concepts
Students may select this if they confuse which event is predictable, perhaps overestimating their ability to drive traffic or misunderstanding legal obligations.

###### Option 3
Both events are equally uncertain and unpredictable — Fails to distinguish certainty from chance
Students may select this if they think all future business events are equally uncertain, missing that some events are deterministic or governed by fixed schedules.

###### Option 4
Neither event can be assigned a probability — Misunderstands probability applicability
Students may select this if they believe probability only applies to games of chance, not recognizing that probability can describe any uncertain event in a business context.
