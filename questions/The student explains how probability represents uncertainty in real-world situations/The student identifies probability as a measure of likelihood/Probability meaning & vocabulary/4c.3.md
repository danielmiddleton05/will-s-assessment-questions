##### Explanation
This question requires students to interpret what a probability value communicates about uncertainty. A probability of 0.03 means there is a 3% chance any individual viewer will click—making it unlikely but not impossible. Across a large number of viewers, the marketing team can reasonably expect some clicks to occur. Probability quantifies how likely an event is without guaranteeing a specific outcome or requiring a minimum threshold to be meaningful. The correct interpretation recognizes probability as a measure of likelihood that acknowledges uncertainty at the individual level while still informing expectations at scale.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
Clicking is virtually impossible because the probability is so close to zero — Confuses "unlikely" with "impossible." This targets students who believe a low probability means the event cannot occur. A probability of 0.03 is small but nonzero, meaning the event is unlikely for any single viewer yet still possible.

###### Option 3
Exactly 3 out of every 100 viewers will definitely click on the ad — Confuses probability with a guaranteed outcome. This targets students who interpret probability as a precise prediction rather than a measure of likelihood. While 0.03 equals 3/100, probability does not guarantee that exactly 3 clicks will occur in every group of 100 viewers.

###### Option 4
The campaign is ineffective because a probability below 0.50 indicates failure — Applies a false threshold to probability. This targets students who believe probability must exceed 0.50 for an event to be meaningful. In many business contexts, such as online advertising, low click-through probabilities are standard and can still represent a successful campaign at scale.
