## Slot 28: quiz
### Task: wgu_a4ds5_Webinar Demo Independence_t7Q96

#### Task Description

A sales team reviews **800** leads from last quarter. For each lead, two events were recorded:

- **W:** The lead attended a live product webinar.  
- **D:** The lead requested a one-on-one product demonstration.

The two-way frequency table below summarizes the results:

|                     | Requested demo (D) | Did not request demo | Total |
|---------------------|-------------------:|---------------------:|------:|
| Attended webinar (W)    | 80                | 240                  | 320   |
| Did not attend webinar  | 120               | 360                  | 480   |
| **Total**               | **200**           | **600**              | **800** |


What conclusion about the relationship between attending a webinar and requesting a demo is best supported by this frequency table?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
These events show a positive association: webinar attendees request demos more often than non-attendees.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
These events are mutually exclusive: a lead can attend a webinar or request a demo, but not both.


###### Option 3 Correct
These events are consistent with independence: the demo-request rate is the same for webinar attendees and non-attendees.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
These events show that attending a webinar causes leads to request demos.

##### Explanation
From the table, the overall probability a lead requests a demo is \(200/800 = 0.25\) (25%).

Among webinar attendees, the probability a lead requests a demo is \(80/320 = 0.25\) (25%).  
Among non-attendees, the probability a lead requests a demo is \(120/480 = 0.25\) (25%).

Because the demo-request rate is the same regardless of webinar attendance, the data are consistent with **independence** between attending a webinar and requesting a demo (no evidence of association in this sample). Options claiming mutual exclusivity or causation are not supported by the table.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This targets students who focus on the raw count “80 requested demos after attending” without comparing rates. The correct comparison is conditional proportions (25% vs 25%), not the size of one cell.

###### Option 2
This targets confusion between “different events” and “mutually exclusive events.” The table shows 80 leads did both, so the events clearly can occur together.

###### Option 4
This targets the common mistake of inferring causation from frequency data. Even if a difference in rates existed, a two-way frequency table alone cannot establish that one event causes the other. Here, the rates are also equal, which further undermines the causal claim.
