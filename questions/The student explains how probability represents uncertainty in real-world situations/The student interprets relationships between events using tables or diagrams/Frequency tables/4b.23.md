#### Task Description

**Given:** A technology company surveys 500 employees about their use of collaboration tools. The frequency table and chart below show the results:

| Tool | Users |
|------|-------|
| Email | 450 |
| Video Calls | 380 |
| Instant Messaging | 420 |
| Project Software | 290 |

Employees may use multiple tools. The survey found that 350 of the employees who use Email also use Instant Messaging.

What does this frequency data suggest about the relationship between using Email and using Instant Messaging?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
These events frequently co-occur in this sample.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
These events are mutually exclusive because they represent different tools.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
These events are independent with no relationship between them.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
These events indicate that Email use causes Instant Messaging adoption.

##### Explanation
Of the 450 Email users, 350 (approximately 78%) also use Instant Messaging. Because the likelihood of an Email user using Instant Messaging (350/450 ≈ 78%) is different from the likelihood of any employee in the survey using Instant Messaging (420/500 = 84%), the events are dependent, meaning a relationship exists between them. Option 2 is incorrect because the problem states 350 employees use both tools; mutual exclusivity would mean no employee could use both. Option 3 is incorrect because the substantial overlap and the difference in proportions (78% vs 84%) indicate these events are not independent. Option 4 is incorrect because frequency data can only show association or co-occurrence, not causation. Both tools might be used together due to other factors such as job requirements or company culture.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
This targets students who confuse "different categories" with "mutually exclusive events." The question explicitly states employees may use multiple tools, and 350 employees demonstrably use both Email and Instant Messaging. Mutual exclusivity would mean no employee could use both simultaneously, which contradicts the given data.

###### Option 3
This targets students who don't recognize that a relationship (dependence) exists when the conditional probability differs from the marginal probability. If Email use and Instant Messaging use were truly independent, 84% of Email users (approx. 378 people) would be expected to use IM. The actual count of 350 shows the events are related.

###### Option 4
This is a common error in statistical reasoning where students confuse correlation with causation. While frequency data can reveal that events are associated or co-occur, it cannot establish that one event causes another. Both tools might be used together due to a third factor (such as job requirements or company culture), not because one directly causes use of the other.
