#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital administrator analyzes patient satisfaction data using the following two-way table:

|            | Satisfied | Not Satisfied | Total |
|------------|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Emergency  | 150       | 50            | 200   |
| Outpatient | 280       | 20            | 300   |
| Total      | 430       | 70            | 500   |


The administrator asks: "Among patients who are satisfied, what proportion came from the Emergency department?"

What type of probability does this question represent?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Marginal probability, because it focuses on the Emergency department row total


###### Option 2 Correct
Conditional probability, because it asks about Emergency department given the patient is satisfied


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Joint probability, because it involves both department and satisfaction status


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Theoretical probability, because it uses the totals from the table structure

##### Explanation
Conditional probability asks about the likelihood of one event given that another event has occurred. The question asks for the probability of "Emergency department" given the condition "satisfied," which requires focusing only on satisfied patients and finding what proportion of them came from Emergency. The key phrase "among patients who are satisfied" indicates that the sample space is restricted to satisfied patients only, making this a conditional probability question.

##### Domain

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Marginal probability, because it focuses on the Emergency department row total — Confuses conditional with marginal
This targets students who don't recognize that the question restricts attention to a subset (satisfied patients only). Marginal probability uses row or column totals relative to the grand total, not conditioned on a specific outcome.

###### Option 3
Joint probability, because it involves both department and satisfaction status — Confuses conditional with joint
This targets students who recognize two variables are involved but don't distinguish between joint probability (the probability of both events occurring together) and conditional probability (the probability of one event given that another has occurred).

###### Option 4
Theoretical probability, because it uses the totals from the table structure — Confuses probability type with data source
This targets students who don't distinguish between the type of probability question being asked (joint, marginal, conditional) and whether the probability is theoretical (based on assumptions of equally likely outcomes) or empirical (based on observed data).
