#### Task Description

A pediatric clinic reviewed 1,000 patient records to study the relationship between flu vaccination status and clinic visits for flu-like symptoms during flu season. The results are organized in the two-way table below.

| Vaccination Status | Visited for Flu Symptoms | No Flu Symptom Visit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vaccinated | 60 | 540 | 600 |
| Not Vaccinated | 120 | 280 | 400 |
| **Total** | **180** | **820** | **1,000** |

A health administrator concludes that flu vaccination status and flu symptom visits are dependent events. How do the data in the table support this conclusion?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
A larger number of unvaccinated patients (120) visited for flu symptoms compared to vaccinated patients (60).


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Flu symptom visits occurred in only 180 out of 1,000 total patients in the study.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The study included a larger vaccinated group (600) than unvaccinated group (400).


###### Option 4 Correct
10% of vaccinated patients visited for flu symptoms, compared to 30% of unvaccinated patients.

##### Explanation
Two events are statistically dependent when the probability of one event changes based on whether the other event has occurred. From the two-way table, the conditional probability of visiting for flu symptoms given vaccination is 60/600 = 10%, while the conditional probability given no vaccination is 120/400 = 30%. Because these conditional rates differ, flu vaccination status and flu symptom visits are dependent events. The correct evidence is the comparison of these conditional rates across the two groups, not raw counts, marginal totals, or group sizes.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
A larger number of unvaccinated patients (120) visited for flu symptoms compared to vaccinated patients (60) — Targets students who confuse raw count differences with rate differences. Because the two groups have different sizes (600 vaccinated vs. 400 unvaccinated), comparing raw counts without adjusting for group size does not properly establish dependence. A student needs to compare conditional rates, not absolute frequencies.

###### Option 2
Flu symptom visits occurred in only 180 out of 1,000 total patients in the study — Targets students who believe the overall (marginal) probability of an event reveals something about its relationship to another event. The marginal rate of 18% describes how common flu visits are overall but says nothing about whether that rate changes across vaccination groups, which is what determines dependence.

###### Option 3
The study included a larger vaccinated group (600) than unvaccinated group (400) — Targets students who confuse differences in sample composition with evidence of a statistical relationship between variables. Unequal group sizes are a feature of the study's data, not an indicator that the probability of one event changes based on the other.
