#### Task Description

**Given:** A diagnostic test for a disease has the following characteristics: when a person has the disease, the test correctly identifies it 95% of the time. However, the test also produces a positive result in 8% of people who do not have the disease.

What does the 8% represent in this context?

##### Explanation
A false positive occurs when the test indicates disease in someone who does not have it. The 8% represents the probability that a healthy person receives a positive (incorrect) test result. This is distinct from false negatives (missing the disease in someone who has it) or overall accuracy.

##### Domain

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The probability of having the disease — Confuses test error with disease prevalence

Students may select this if they confuse the probability of a test error with the probability of actually having the disease in the population.

###### Option 3
The probability of a correct test result — Reverses the meaning

Students may select this if they do not distinguish between correct results and errors, or if they misread what the 8% refers to.

###### Option 4
The probability of a false negative result — Confuses types of test errors

Students may select this if they confuse false positives (positive test when healthy) with false negatives (negative test when diseased).
