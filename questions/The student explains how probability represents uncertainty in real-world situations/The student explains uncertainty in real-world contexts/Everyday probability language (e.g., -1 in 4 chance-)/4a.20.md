##### Explanation
"1 in 5" is everyday language expressing a probability of $\frac{1}{5} = 0.20 = 20\%$. This means approximately 20% of students receive merit scholarships on average, not that exact patterns occur among specific groups of students.

##### Domain

Education

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
The probability of a student receiving a scholarship increases if the previous students sampled did not receive one — Gambler's Fallacy

This targets the common misconception that independent events are self-correcting. Students may believe that if a "success" (scholarship) hasn't occurred in a few trials, it becomes more likely to occur in the next trial.

###### Option 3
Students have a 5% chance of receiving a merit scholarship — Incorrectly converts the ratio

This targets students who misinterpret "1 in 5" as $1\% \times 5 = 5\%$ rather than correctly calculating $\frac{1}{5} = 20\%$.

###### Option 4
For every 5 students who do not receive a merit scholarship, there is 1 student who does — Confuses probability with odds

This targets students who interpret "1 in 5" as a ratio of 1:5 (successes to failures). A 1:5 odds ratio actually represents a 1 in 6 probability (approximately 16.7%), whereas "1 in 5" represents a 1 in 5 probability (20%).
