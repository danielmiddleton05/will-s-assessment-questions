#### Task Description

**Given:** A manufacturer reports that their electronic component has a 2% failure rate within the first year. A purchasing manager is ordering 500 components for a business project.

What does the 2% failure rate indicate about the risk and uncertainty involved in this purchase?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Since the outcome is uncertain, it is just as likely that 50 components will fail as it is that 10 will fail.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
All 500 components will perform identically and either all fail or all succeed together.


###### Option 3 Correct
Approximately 10 components may fail, but the exact number cannot be predicted with certainty.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
No components will fail because 2% represents a very low failure rate.

##### Explanation
A 2% failure rate means approximately 2% of components are expected to fail (0.02 × 500 = 10). However, probability describes likelihood, not certainty—the actual number of failures could be somewhat higher or lower than 10 due to random variation inherent in any probabilistic process.

##### Domain

Business

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Since the outcome is uncertain, it is just as likely that 50 components will fail as it is that 10 will fail — Misinterprets probability distribution and variance
This targets students who acknowledge uncertainty but incorrectly believe it means all outcomes are equally probable, missing that probability distributions cluster around an expected value (mean).

###### Option 2
All 500 components will perform identically and either all fail or all succeed together — Misunderstands independence of components
This targets students who don't recognize that each component's performance is independent, so some may fail while others succeed.

###### Option 4
No components will fail because 2% represents a very low failure rate — Equates low probability with impossibility
This targets students who interpret low probability as meaning an event won't occur, missing that some failures are still expected even with low rates.
