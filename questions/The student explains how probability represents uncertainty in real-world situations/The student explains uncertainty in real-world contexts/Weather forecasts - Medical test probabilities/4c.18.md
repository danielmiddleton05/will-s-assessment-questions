#### Task Description

A weather service reports: "There is a 60% probability of thunderstorms Friday evening."

What does this probability represent regarding the likelihood of thunderstorms occurring Friday evening?

##### Explanation
A 60% probability of thunderstorms means that based on current atmospheric conditions and weather models, there is a 60% chance storms will develop—and a 40% chance they won't. This probability quantifies the uncertainty inherent in weather prediction. It communicates that storms are more likely than not, but the outcome is not guaranteed either way. The probability is a measure of how uncertain the forecast is, not a promise of a specific result.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
It means thunderstorms will occur for 60% of Friday evening, with clear skies for the remaining 40% — Misinterprets probability as time distribution
This targets students who confuse probability with a proportion of time. They may incorrectly think 60% probability means storms for 60% of the evening, rather than understanding it as a measure of likelihood for the entire period.

###### Option 2
It guarantees thunderstorms will happen because the probability exceeds 50% — Treats probability as binary guarantee
This targets students who mistakenly believe that exceeding 50% probability converts likelihood into certainty. They fail to recognize that any probability less than 100% still represents uncertainty about the outcome.

###### Option 4
It describes the forecasters' confidence level rather than actual likelihood of storms occurring — Separates confidence from likelihood
This targets students who may think probability only reflects subjective feelings of the forecasters rather than understanding that probability quantifies the actual uncertainty about whether storms will occur based on atmospheric data and models.
