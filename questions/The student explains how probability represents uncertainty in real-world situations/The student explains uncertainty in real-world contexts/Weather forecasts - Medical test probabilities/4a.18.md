**Given:** A medical screening test correctly classifies approximately 85% of patients in a population similar to the one being tested.

##### Explanation
An 85% accuracy rate in a population means the test is generally reliable, but it does not mean there is a fixed 15% error rate for any single patient. Because individual risk factors and base rates vary, the test result must be interpreted within the broader medical context, reflecting residual uncertainty.

##### Domain

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The result should be treated as definitive because the test is correct most of the time — Confuses high probability with certainty
This targets students who don't recognize that any probability less than 100% means some uncertainty remains, regardless of how "high" the probability seems.

###### Option 2
There is exactly a 15% chance that this individual patient’s result is incorrect — Misinterprets population accuracy as an individual probability guarantee
This targets students who mistakenly apply a population-level accuracy metric directly as an exact personal error rate without considering individual clinical context.

###### Option 4
The accuracy rate cannot provide any useful information about individual test results — Incorrectly limits probability's applicability
This targets students who may think probability only describes group behavior and provides no useful signal for interpreting individual cases.
