#### Task Description

**Given:** A company surveyed 400 business clients about service quality. Of these clients, 240 rated the service as "excellent" or "good," 100 rated the service as "fair," and the remaining clients rated the service as "poor."

What is the relative frequency of clients who rated the service as "poor"?

##### Explanation
To find the relative frequency of "poor" ratings, the number of "poor" responses must first be determined. Since the total is 400, and 240 were "excellent/good" and 100 were "fair," the number of "poor" ratings is $400 - 240 - 100 = 60$. The relative frequency is then calculated by dividing this count by the total number of observations: $60 \div 400 = 0.15$. This represents the proportion of clients who rated the service as "poor," expressed as a decimal.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
Calculates the relative frequency of the "fair" category ($100 \div 400 = 0.25$) instead of the "poor" category. This targets students who select the count for the wrong rating category and divide it by the total without recognizing it does not correspond to the group specified in the question.

###### Option 3
Subtracts only the "excellent/good" group from the total ($400 - 240 = 160$) and then divides by 400 ($160 \div 400 = 0.40$). This targets students who perform only one subtraction step, failing to also remove the "fair" ratings and effectively treating all non-positive responses as "poor."

###### Option 4
Calculates the relative frequency of the "excellent" or "good" ratings ($240 \div 400 = 0.60$). This targets students who compute the relative frequency of the largest complementary group rather than the specific category requested in the question.
