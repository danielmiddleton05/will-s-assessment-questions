#### Task Description

**Given:** A streaming service recorded that 90 out of 600 video streams experienced buffering interruptions during a trial period. The service expects 2,000 streams next month.

Based on this data, approximately how many of the 2,000 expected streams would be predicted to experience buffering interruptions?

##### Explanation
First, calculate the empirical probability of a buffering interruption from the trial data:
$$P(\text{buffering}) = \frac{90}{600} = 0.15$$
Then, apply this probability to the expected number of future streams to estimate the frequency of buffering events:
$$\text{Expected buffering streams} = 0.15 \times 2{,}000 = 300$$
This two-step process uses the relative frequency from observed data to predict how often an event is likely to occur in a future sample, which is a core application of empirical probability.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
200 — This results from incorrectly simplifying the fraction 90/600 by dividing the numerator and denominator by different factors (90 ÷ 9 = 10 and 600 ÷ 6 = 100), arriving at 10/100 = 0.10 instead of the correct 3/20 = 0.15. Multiplying 0.10 × 2,000 = 200. This targets students who make errors in fraction simplification by failing to divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same value.

###### Option 2
270 — This results from truncating the scaling factor to a whole number. The student correctly identifies that the new sample is 2,000/600 = 10/3 ≈ 3.33 times larger, but rounds down to 3, then computes 90 × 3 = 270. This targets students who approximate the scaling factor by truncating rather than computing the exact ratio.

###### Option 4
360 — This results from rounding the scaling factor up to the next whole number. The student computes 600 × 3 = 1,800, sees this falls short of 2,000, and rounds up to a multiplier of 4, then computes 90 × 4 = 360. This targets students who default to whole-number multipliers rather than working with the precise fractional scaling factor of 10/3.
