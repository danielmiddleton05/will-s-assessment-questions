#### Task Description

**Given:** A tech startup tracked the daily number of app crashes across 9 testing days. The values, sorted from least to greatest, are: 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 40.

An analyst reported the mean as 8.0 crashes per day and the median as 4 crashes per day.

Why is the mean significantly higher than the median for this dataset?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The repeated value of 3 pulls the median lower, but repeated values do not change the mean calculation.


###### Option 2 Correct
The outlier of 40 increases the total sum and mean, while the median only reflects the middle position.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The mean is higher because it accounts for every value, whereas the median ignores most of the data.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The median is found by averaging only the smallest and largest values, so the low value of 1 pulls it downward.

##### Explanation
The mean is calculated by summing all values and dividing by the count: (1 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 8 + 40) / 9 = 72 / 9 = 8.0. Because the mean uses every value in the dataset, the outlier of 40 increases the total sum, pulling the mean well above most of the data. The median is the middle value in the sorted list. With 9 values, the median is the 5th value, which is 4. Because the median depends only on position rather than magnitude, the extreme value of 40 has no effect on it. This is why the mean (8.0) is significantly higher than the median (4)—the outlier raises the mean but leaves the median unchanged.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
This distractor targets students who attribute the discrepancy to repeated values rather than the outlier. While the value 3 appears twice, repeated values affect both the mean and the median in the same general way; they do not create the kind of asymmetric pull that an outlier does. A repeated value is included in the sum for the mean and also occupies positions in the sorted list that determine the median.

###### Option 3
This distractor targets students who believe the mean is naturally larger because its calculation involves every number in the set. While the mean does use all values, this does not automatically make it larger than the median; the relationship between the two measures is determined by the distribution and skew of the data, not the inclusive nature of the mean's formula.

###### Option 4
This distractor targets students who confuse the median with the midrange. The midrange is calculated by averaging the minimum and maximum values: (1 + 40) / 2 = 20.5. The median, however, is the middle positional value in a sorted dataset and does not use the smallest and largest values in its computation. This misconception conflates two distinct measures of center.
