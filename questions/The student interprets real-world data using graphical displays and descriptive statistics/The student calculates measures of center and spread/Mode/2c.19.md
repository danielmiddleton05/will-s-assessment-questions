#### Task Description

A clinic administrator reviewed 25 patient records to determine how many times each patient visited the emergency department during the past year. The raw data for the number of visits is as follows:

1, 3, 2, 5, 1, 4, 3, 1, 3, 2, 5, 1, 3, 4, 1, 3, 2, 4, 1, 3, 2, 4, 5, 5, 5

What is the correct descripton of the mode of emergency department visits for this patient group?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
The distribution is bimodal: the values 1 and 3 both occur with the highest frequency of six times each.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The distribution is unimodal with a mode of 3 because when two values tie for the highest frequency, the larger value is the mode.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The distribution is bimodal with modes of 1 and 5 because they are the smallest and largest values in the dataset.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The distribution has no mode because more than one value shares the highest frequency of six occurrences.

##### Explanation
To find the mode, the student must first calculate the frequency of each value in the raw data set. 
- 1 visit: 6 occurrences
- 2 visits: 4 occurrences
- 3 visits: 6 occurrences
- 4 visits: 4 occurrences
- 5 visits: 5 occurrences
The mode is the value that appears most frequently. Since both 1 and 3 appear 6 times (the highest frequency), the data set is bimodal with modes of 1 and 3.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
The distribution is unimodal with a mode of 3... — This targets candidates who correctly count the frequencies and identify the tie between 1 and 3 but incorrectly believe a dataset can have only one mode and apply a tiebreaker rule such as choosing the larger value.

###### Option 3
The distribution is bimodal with modes of 1 and 5... — This targets candidates who confuse the concept of mode with the range of the data, incorrectly selecting the minimum and maximum values rather than the values with the highest frequency.

###### Option 4
The distribution has no mode... — This targets candidates who correctly count the frequencies and find a tie but incorrectly believe that when two or more values share the highest frequency, the distribution has no mode.
