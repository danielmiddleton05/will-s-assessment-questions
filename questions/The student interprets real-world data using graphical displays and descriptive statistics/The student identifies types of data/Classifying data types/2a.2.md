##### Explanation
The number of customers is quantitative discrete data. It is quantitative because it represents a numerical measurement that can be used in mathematical operations. It is discrete because customers can only be counted in whole numbers—you cannot have 2.5 customers. Discrete data have gaps between possible values, while continuous data can take any value within a range.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Quantitative continuous — Confuses discrete and continuous data

This is the most common error. Students may recognize that the data is quantitative but fail to distinguish between discrete (countable, whole numbers) and continuous (measurable, any value in a range) data. Continuous data examples include height, weight, or temperature.

###### Option 2
Categorical nominal — Confuses quantitative and categorical data

This targets students who may not recognize that numerical counts are quantitative data. Nominal data are categories without inherent order, such as store location type or product brand.

###### Option 3
Categorical ordinal — Confuses quantitative data with ranked categories

This targets students who confuse counted numerical data with ordered categorical data. Ordinal data represent ranked categories, such as satisfaction ratings (low, medium, high) or education levels.
