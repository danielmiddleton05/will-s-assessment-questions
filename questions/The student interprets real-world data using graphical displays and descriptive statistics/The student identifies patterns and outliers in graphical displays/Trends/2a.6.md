##### Explanation
Despite month-to-month fluctuations typical of project-based work, the revenue values show an overall increase from \$82,000 in January to \$116,000 in December, demonstrating a consistent upward pattern over the year. An increasing trend is characterized by values that generally rise over time, even when short-term variations exist.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Seasonal trend — Misinterprets monthly fluctuations as a recurring pattern linked to the time of year.
Students may select this if they focus on the periodic rises and falls (the "peaks and valleys") typical of project-based work, failing to see the overall upward slope of the data set across the entire year.

###### Option 2
Decreasing trend — Reverses the direction of change.
Students may select this if they misread the graph direction, start from the wrong end of the data, or confuse increasing with decreasing. This distractor targets understanding of basic trend terminology and directionality.

###### Option 4
No trend — Denies the consistent pattern due to noise. 
Students selecting this may focus exclusively on the month-to-month fluctuations (volatility) and conclude that because the values do not move in a perfectly straight line, no identifiable trend exists. This distractor tests the ability to distinguish between random variance and a directional trend.
