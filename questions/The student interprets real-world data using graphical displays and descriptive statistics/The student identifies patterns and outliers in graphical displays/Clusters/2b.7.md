#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student interprets real-world data using graphical displays and descriptive statistics.

##### Evidence Statement
The student identifies patterns and outliers in graphical displays.

##### Aligned Content
Clusters

##### Explanation
The scatter plot displays two distinct groups of data points that are concentrated in specific areas (younger low-spenders and older high-spenders) with a gap between them. This is the defining characteristic of clustering—data points that group together in separate concentrations rather than being spread evenly or following a continuous pattern.

##### Domain
Business

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Students who select this may confuse clusters with outliers. An outlier is a single point (or a few points) that falls far from the main body of data. In contrast, clusters are groups of multiple points concentrated together. The scenario describes two entire groups of points, which represents clustering, not a single unusual value.

###### Option 2
Students who select this may recognize a general upward direction but fail to notice the break in data. A continuous linear trend would show a steady progression of points forming a single line across the whole range, rather than two separate concentrations with no data in between.

###### Option 4
Students who select this don't recognize that the presence of two separate groups with a gap between them is the opposite of uniform distribution. A uniform spread would show data points scattered relatively evenly across all values, not concentrated in two specific areas with very few points in between.
