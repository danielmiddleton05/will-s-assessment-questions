##### Aligned Content
Shape (skewed)

##### Explanation
A distribution is skewed left (negatively skewed) when the tail extends toward lower values while most data clusters at higher values. This is common with exam scores on relatively easy tests, where most employees score near the maximum while only a few earn significantly lower scores, creating a long left tail.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Symmetric — Ignores the tail description

A symmetric distribution has roughly equal frequencies on both sides of the center with no extended tail in either direction. The description clearly indicates an extended tail toward lower values. Students who focus on the center of the distribution rather than the tail direction may select this option.

###### Option 2
Bimodal — Misidentifies the number of peaks

A bimodal distribution has two distinct peaks, often occurring when data comes from two different subgroups. While exam data could be bimodal if there were two distinct groups of learners, the histogram shows a single peak with a long tail toward lower values, not two separate peaks.

###### Option 3
Skewed right — Reverses skewness direction

In a right-skewed distribution, the tail extends toward higher values. Students who confuse the direction of skewness or don't remember that skewness is named for the direction of the tail might select this. This is a common error where students mistakenly identify the skew based on the location of the "hump" (the mode) rather than the tail.
