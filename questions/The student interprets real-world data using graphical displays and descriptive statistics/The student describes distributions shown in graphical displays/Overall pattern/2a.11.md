##### Explanation
A bimodal distribution is characterized by two distinct peaks or modes, indicating that the data clusters around two different values. The histogram shows two clear peaks—one around 150 cups and another around 350 cups—with a gap between them, which is the defining characteristic of a bimodal pattern. This suggests two different types of days (perhaps weekdays vs. weekends, or regular days vs. special event days).

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The distribution is unimodal and centered at 250 cups — Misinterprets the shape of the data. While 250 is the midpoint of the range, the distribution is not unimodal because there is no peak at this value; in fact, the frequency at 250 is at its lowest point. This distractor targets students who confuse the center of a range with the peak (mode) of a distribution.

###### Option 3
The distribution is skewed to the left and right — Misapplies the concept of skewness. Skewness describes a distribution with a long tail on one side; a single distribution is typically not described as being skewed in both directions simultaneously. This distractor targets students who may see the data spread away from the center (250) toward the two peaks and misidentify this symmetric spread as dual skewness rather than recognizing the presence of two distinct modes.

###### Option 4
The distribution is uniform across the range of sales volumes — Fails to recognize the variation in frequency. A uniform distribution would show approximately equal heights for all bars between 100 and 400. The distinct peaks and the deep valley between them indicate a non-uniform pattern.
