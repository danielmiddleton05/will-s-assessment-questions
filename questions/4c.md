###### Option 4 Incorrect

#### Quiz Settings



#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student explains how probability represents uncertainty in real-world situations.

##### Evidence Statement
The student estimates event frequency from real or simulated data.

##### Aligned Content
Law of large numbers (conceptual)

##### Domain
Technology

##### Explanation
The law of large numbers states that as the number of trials increases, the observed proportion of outcomes converges toward the theoretical (true) probability. With only 40 users, each individual crash report represents 2.5% of the total, meaning random variation can easily push the observed rate far from the true crash rate. As thousands of users are added, each individual result contributes a much smaller share to the overall proportion, and the cumulative effect of random fluctuations diminishes. This allows the observed crash rate to stabilize near the update's actual crash rate, giving the team a far more reliable basis for their decision.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
This targets the gambler's fallacy — the misconception that future independent outcomes will "correct" or compensate for unusual early results. In reality, future crash reports do not respond to what happened earlier. The observed rate improves not because later results counteract early ones, but because the early results become an increasingly small portion of the growing dataset, reducing their influence on the overall proportion.

###### Option 2
This targets a misapplication of the normal distribution (empirical rule). While large datasets may approximate a normal distribution in certain analyses, the stabilization of the observed crash rate is explained by the law of large numbers, not by data forming a bell-curve shape. The normal distribution describes how data values spread around a mean, not why an observed proportion converges to a true probability over many trials.

###### Option 4
This targets the plausible but incorrect idea that hardware diversity is the primary statistical reason for improved accuracy. While testing across varied devices may be a valid practical concern, it does not explain the fundamental statistical phenomenon at work. Even if every user had the identical device, collecting data from more users would still produce a more stable proportion due to the law of large numbers reducing the impact of random variation.

Students who partially understand dependency but do not distinguish it from mutual exclusivity may find this appealing.

Two events are statistically dependent when the probability of one event changes based on whether the other event has occurred. From the two-way table, the conditional probability of visiting for flu symptoms given vaccination is 60/600 = 10%, while the conditional probability given no vaccination is 120/400 = 30%. Because these conditional rates differ, flu vaccination status and flu symptom visits are dependent events.
