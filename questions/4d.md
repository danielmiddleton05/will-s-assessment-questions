### Task: wgu_a4ds1_Identifying Sample Space Structure_r7Nm3

#### Task Description

**Given:** A data center runs an automated backup every night. Each backup produces one of three statuses: "complete," "partial," or "failed."

What does the set {$\text{complete, partial, failed}$} represent within the probability structure of a single nightly backup?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
It is the sample space because it lists every possible result of a single backup.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
It is an event because it describes a collection of outcomes that could occur.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
It is a trial because it represents three separate attempts at backing up data.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
It is a probability distribution because it identifies how likely each status is to occur.

##### Explanation
The sample space is the complete set of all possible outcomes of a probability experiment. For a single nightly backup, the three possible results are "complete," "partial," and "failed." Because $\{\text{complete, partial, failed}\}$ exhaustively lists every result that could occur from one backup, it is the sample space. An event is a subset of the sample space (e.g., $\{\text{partial, failed}\}$ might represent the event "the backup did not fully succeed"). A trial is the act of performing the experiment—in this case, running one nightly backup—not the list of possible results. A probability distribution would assign a numerical probability to each outcome, but the given set contains only labels with no associated likelihoods.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
"It is an event because it describes a collection of outcomes that could occur" — Confuses an event with the sample space. While an event is indeed a set of outcomes, the defining feature of the sample space is that it contains all possible outcomes. A student who does not distinguish between a proper subset and the full set of outcomes may select this option.

###### Option 3
"It is a trial because it represents three separate attempts at backing up data" — Confuses three listed outcomes with three separate attempts. A trial is the single act of performing the experiment (running one backup), not the enumeration of its possible results. A student who misreads the three statuses as three repetitions of the process may find this plausible.

###### Option 4
"It is a probability distribution because it identifies how likely each status is to occur" — Confuses a sample space with a probability distribution. A probability distribution assigns a numerical likelihood to each outcome; the given set merely names the outcomes without any associated probabilities. A student who conflates listing outcomes with quantifying their chances may choose this option.

#### Task Description

**Given:** Over the past five semesters, a college tutoring center recorded that 126 out of 180 students who attended weekly tutoring sessions passed their math course. Based on this data, the center reports that the probability a student attending weekly tutoring will pass is 0.70.

What characteristic of this estimate makes it an example of empirical probability?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
It is calculated from the frequency of outcomes observed in actual student records.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
It assumes each student attending tutoring has an equally likely chance of passing.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
It was derived from a mathematical model of all possible academic outcomes.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
It will produce the same value each time regardless of which student records are examined.

#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student explains how probability represents uncertainty in real-world situations.

##### Evidence Statement
The student estimates event frequency from real or simulated data.

##### Aligned Content
Observational (empirical) probability (conceptual)

##### Domain
Education

##### Explanation
Empirical probability is defined by its reliance on observed data from real-world events. In this scenario, the tutoring center divided the number of students who passed (126) by the total number of students tracked (180), yielding 0.70. This estimate is empirical because it is derived directly from the frequency of outcomes recorded in actual student data rather than from theoretical assumptions or mathematical models. The defining characteristic is its basis in observed results.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
It assumes each student attending tutoring has an equally likely chance of passing — Describes a characteristic of theoretical probability.
Students may select this if they confuse the basis of empirical probability with the assumptions underlying theoretical probability, where outcomes are treated as equally likely rather than derived from observed frequencies.

###### Option 3
It was derived from a mathematical model of all possible academic outcomes — Describes a theoretical approach to probability.
Students may select this if they believe that any calculation involving division of counts constitutes a mathematical model, failing to distinguish between analyzing observed data and constructing a model of all possible outcomes.

###### Option 4
It will produce the same value each time regardless of which student records are examined — Reflects a misconception about empirical probability.
Students may select this if they believe empirical probability yields a fixed, universal constant. In reality, empirical probability can change depending on the specific data collected, since different samples of students or semesters may produce different observed frequencies.

#### Task Description

A hospital pharmacy tracks adverse drug reactions (ADRs) to a commonly prescribed antibiotic. The manufacturer's clinical trial of 45,000 patients established an ADR rate of 8%. How should the pharmacy interpret the difference between its observed 17% ADR rate and the manufacturer's established 8% rate?

###### Option 2 Incorrect
As confirmation that patient reactions are independent events, making the pharmacy's 30-patient result equally reliable as the manufacturer's trial.


###### Option 3 Correct
As a result likely driven by the small patient sample, where the observed rate should stabilize closer to 8% as more patients are tracked.


###### Option 4 Incorrect

#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student explains how probability represents uncertainty in real-world situations.

##### Evidence Statement
The student estimates event frequency from real or simulated data.

##### Aligned Content
Law of large numbers (conceptual)

##### Domain
Healthcare

##### Explanation
The law of large numbers states that as the number of observations increases, the observed proportion converges toward the true probability. The pharmacy's sample of only 30 patients is highly susceptible to random variation, which can produce an observed ADR rate (17%) that deviates substantially from the established rate (8%). As the pharmacy tracks more patients over time, the observed rate is expected to move closer to the manufacturer's 8% figure. The current discrepancy does not constitute reliable evidence that the drug is more dangerous than reported; it reflects the natural instability of proportions calculated from small samples.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This targets students who prioritize the recency of data over the size of the sample. It incorrectly suggests that a small, local sample (n=30) can invalidate the results of a massive, rigorous clinical trial (n=45,000) simply because the data was collected more recently.

###### Option 2
This targets the concept of independence. While it is true that each patient's reaction is an independent event, independence does not mean a 30-patient sample is equally reliable as a 45,000-patient trial. The law of large numbers explains that larger samples produce more stable and accurate estimates of the true proportion. A student selecting this option confuses the idea of independent trials with the precision of an estimate.

###### Option 4
This targets the misconception of linear trends in probability. It suggests that a deviation from the expected rate indicates a continuing upward trend, whereas the law of large numbers dictates that larger samples will lead to a stabilization of the rate toward the true probability (8%) rather than a predictable, compounding increase.

What conclusion about the relationship between attending a webinar and requesting a demo is best supported by this frequency table?

###### Option 2 Incorrect
These events are mutually exclusive: a lead can attend a webinar or request a demo, but not both.


###### Option 3 Correct

##### Explanation
From the table, the overall probability a lead requests a demo is \(200/800 = 0.25\) (25%).

Among webinar attendees, the probability a lead requests a demo is \(80/320 = 0.25\) (25%).

###### Option 4
This targets the common mistake of inferring causation from frequency data. Even if a difference in rates existed, a two-way frequency table alone cannot establish that one event causes the other. Here, the rates are also equal, which further undermines the causal claim.
