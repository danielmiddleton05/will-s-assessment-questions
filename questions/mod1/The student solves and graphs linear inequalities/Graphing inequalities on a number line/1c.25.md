##### Explanation
The scenario describes that temperatures "below 15°C" trigger an alert, which translates to t < 15. The explicit statement that "at exactly 15°C, no alert is triggered" confirms that 15 is NOT included in the solution set, requiring an open circle. Since the alert triggers for temperatures below 15, the shading extends to the left (toward smaller values). This variation requires students to translate verbal constraints into graphical representation, a skill essential for interpreting system thresholds in technology monitoring applications.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
Targets students who correctly identify the direction of shading (left, toward smaller values) but incorrectly use a closed circle. This error indicates misunderstanding that "below 15°C" means strictly less than, not less than or equal to. Despite the explicit statement that 15°C does not trigger an alert, students may overlook this detail or confuse "below" with "at or below."

###### Option 3
Targets students who correctly use an open circle but shade in the wrong direction, representing t > 15 instead of t < 15. This reflects confusion about interpreting "drops below" as meaning temperatures greater than the threshold rather than less than. Students may misread the directional language in the scenario.

###### Option 4
Targets students who make both errors: using a closed circle and shading in the wrong direction, representing t ≥ 15 instead of t < 15. This reflects fundamental misunderstanding of both the boundary inclusion and the inequality direction from the verbal description, possibly confusing alert conditions with safe operating conditions.
