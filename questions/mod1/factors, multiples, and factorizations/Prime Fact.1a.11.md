**Given:** A data encryption algorithm requires expressing the number $180$ as a product of its prime factors.

##### Explanation
$180 = 2 \times 90 = 2 \times 2 \times 45 = 2 \times 2 \times 9 \times 5 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5$. All factors in this expression are prime numbers ($2$, $3$, and $5$), and the exponents indicate how many times each prime factor appears in the complete factorization.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Targets students who make errors in counting factor occurrences. The calculation $2^3 \times 3 \times 5 = 8 \times 3 \times 5 = 120$, not $180$. This reflects miscounting prime factors—the exponent on $2$ is too high and the exponent on $3$ is too low.

###### Option 3
Targets students who incorrectly determine exponents during factorization. The calculation $2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 = 2 \times 9 \times 25 = 450$, not $180$. This reflects undercounting the factor of $2$ while overcounting the factor of $5$.

###### Option 4
Targets students who add an extra factor of $2$ during the factorization process. The calculation $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 8 \times 9 \times 5 = 360$, not $180$. While this expression correctly uses only prime factors, the exponent on $2$ is too high.
