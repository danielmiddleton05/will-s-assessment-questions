##### Explanation
29 is prime because its only factors are 1 and 29. The other options are all composite numbers: $27 = 3 \times 9$ (or $3^3$), $33 = 3 \times 11$, and $39 = 3 \times 13$. Prime numbers have exactly two distinct factors (1 and the number itself), which makes them essential in cryptographic applications for securing statistical databases.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
27 is a composite number ($3 \times 9$ or $3^3$). Students may select this thinking all odd numbers are prime, or may not recognize it as a perfect cube. The number appears "prime-like" to those who don't test divisibility by small primes.

###### Option 3
33 is a composite number ($3 \times 11$). The repeated digit pattern (3 and 3) might seem to indicate prime properties. Students may not immediately recognize that 33 is divisible by 3, despite the obvious digit sum divisibility rule.

###### Option 4
39 is a composite number ($3 \times 13$). Students may not quickly identify that 39 is divisible by 3, as applying the divisibility rule requires calculating the digit sum (3 + 9 = 12, which is divisible by 3). This number is far enough from common multiples that it may appear prime without careful analysis.
