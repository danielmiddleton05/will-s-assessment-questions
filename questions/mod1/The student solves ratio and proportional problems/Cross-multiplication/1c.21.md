##### Explanation
To maintain the same tutor-to-student ratio, the proportion $\frac{5}{80} = \frac{x}{128}$ must hold. Cross-multiplying gives $5 \times 128 = 80 \times x$, so $640 = 80x$, and therefore $x = \frac{640}{80} = 8$. Alternatively, the enrollment increased by a factor of $\frac{128}{80} = 1.6$, so the number of tutors must also increase by the same factor: $5 \times 1.6 = 8$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Targets the student who calculates only the additional tutors needed rather than the total. The student correctly finds the increase in students ($128 - 80 = 48$) and sets up a proportion for only the additional students: $\frac{5}{80} = \frac{x}{48}$. Cross-multiplying gives $x = \frac{5 \times 48}{80} = 3$. The student reports 3 as the total tutors needed, not recognizing that 3 represents only the additional tutors and the correct total is $5 + 3 = 8$.

###### Option 2
Targets the student who sets up the correct proportion $\frac{5}{80} = \frac{x}{128}$ and attempts cross-multiplication but makes an arithmetic error when computing $5 \times 128$, arriving at 560 instead of 640. This leads to $x = \frac{560}{80} = 7$.

###### Option 4
Targets the student who estimates the scaling factor rather than computing it exactly. The student approximates $\frac{128}{80}$ as 2 instead of the precise value of 1.6, then multiplies $5 \times 2 = 10$. This reflects an error in which the student rounds the ratio before applying it, inflating the result.
