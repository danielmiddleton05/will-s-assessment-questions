#### Task Description

**Given:** A marketing analyst is scaling customer engagement data from a sample of 25 customers to a larger group of 40 customers. In the original sample, 15 customers were actively engaged.

If the engagement rate remains constant when scaling to the larger group, how many customers would be actively engaged?

##### Explanation
To maintain the same engagement rate, the proportion $\frac{15}{25} = \frac{x}{40}$ must hold. Since the group size increased from 25 to 40 (a factor of $\frac{40}{25} = 1.6$), the number of engaged customers must also increase by the same factor: $15 \times 1.6 = 24$. Alternatively, cross-multiplying gives $15 \times 40 = 25 \times x$, so $x = \frac{600}{25} = 24$.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Targets the student who applies inverse proportional reasoning. The student incorrectly assumes that as the total group size increases, fewer customers proportionally would be engaged, calculating $\frac{15 \times 25}{40} = 9.375$. This reflects a misunderstanding that direct proportions require quantities to scale in the same direction.

###### Option 2
Targets the student who confuses which quantity to use in the proportion. The student calculates the number of non-engaged customers in the original sample ($25 - 15 = 10$) and mistakenly uses this value in the proportion: $\frac{10}{25} = \frac{x}{40}$. Cross-multiplying gives $x = \frac{10 \times 40}{25} = 16$. The student reports this as the number of engaged customers, when it actually represents the scaled number of non-engaged customers.

###### Option 4
Targets the student who inverts the proportional relationship. The student sets up $\frac{25}{x} = \frac{15}{40}$, placing the totals in one ratio and engaged customers in another, leading to $x = \frac{25 \times 40}{15} \approx 66.67$.
