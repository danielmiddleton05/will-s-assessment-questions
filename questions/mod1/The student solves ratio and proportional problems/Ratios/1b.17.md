##### Explanation
To find the doctor-to-patient ratio in simplest form, start with 4:20. Find the greatest common factor (GCF) of both terms, which is 4. Divide both terms by the GCF: 4 ÷ 4 = 1 and 20 ÷ 4 = 5. This yields the simplified ratio of 1:5, meaning for every 1 doctor, the clinic sees 5 patients per day.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
Presents the original unsimplified ratio. Targets students who recognize the correct relationship between doctors and patients but fail to reduce the ratio to simplest form as required by the question.

###### Option 3
Results from dividing by an incorrect factor. Targets students who attempt to simplify but divide 20 by 5 instead of by 4, or who incorrectly identify the GCF, yielding an incorrect simplification of 1:4.

###### Option 4
Inverts the ratio order. Targets students who correctly simplify to obtain the values 1 and 5 but reverse the sequence, confusing which quantity (doctors or patients) should be listed first in the ratio.
