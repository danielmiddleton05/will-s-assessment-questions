#### Task Description

A small business owner manages monthly finances in two steps. First, the owner pays $1,500 in fixed expenses from the month's total revenue. Then, the owner deposits one-fifth of the remaining amount into a savings account. Last month, the savings deposit was $640.

What was the business's total revenue last month?

##### Explanation
The relationship can be expressed as $(1/5)(R - 1{,}500) = 640$, where $R$ is the total revenue. To isolate $R$, first multiply both sides by 5: $R - 1{,}500 = 3{,}200$. Then add $1{,}500$ to both sides: $R = 3{,}200 + 1{,}500 = 4{,}700$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Adds the savings deposit and fixed expenses without accounting for the one-fifth relationship: $640 + 1{,}500 = 2{,}140$. This targets students who recognize both values are relevant but simply combine them, treating the problem as Revenue = Savings + Expenses. They fail to set up or solve the equation, ignoring that $640 represents only one-fifth of what remains after expenses.

###### Option 2
Multiplies the savings by 5 but does not add back the fixed expenses: $640 \times 5 = 3{,}200$. This targets students who correctly undo the one-fifth relationship but stop short of the full solution, forgetting that fixed expenses were subtracted before the savings calculation. They treat the equation as $(1/5)(R) = 640$ rather than $(1/5)(R - 1{,}500) = 640$.

###### Option 4
Adds the fixed expenses to the savings first, then multiplies by 5: $(640 + 1{,}500) \times 5 = 2{,}140 \times 5 = 10{,}700$. This targets students who identify both necessary operations but apply them in the wrong order. Instead of first multiplying by 5 and then adding $1{,}500$, they reverse the sequence, demonstrating confusion about the order of inverse operations when isolating a variable.
