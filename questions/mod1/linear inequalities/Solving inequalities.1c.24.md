#### Task Description

**Given:** A college program requires students to complete at least 150 hours of practicum experience to graduate. A student has already completed 45 hours through a summer internship, and plans to participate in weekly practicum sessions that last 6 hours each.

What is the minimum number of weekly sessions needed to meet the graduation requirement?

##### Explanation
To solve this inequality, first isolate the variable term: $45 + 6s \ge 150$. Subtract $45$ from both sides: $6s \ge 105$. Divide both sides by $6$: $s \ge 17.5$. Since $s$ must be a whole number and the inequality requires at least $17.5$ sessions, the minimum number of whole sessions needed is $18$ (rounding up to ensure the requirement is met). Rounding down to $17$ would yield only $45 + 6(17) = 147$ hours, which falls short of the $150$-hour requirement.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
Results from correctly setting up and solving the inequality ($s \ge 17.5$) and rounding up to $18$, but then incorrectly adding one more session to get $19$. This targets students who misinterpret the minimum requirement, believing that "at least" means the answer must strictly exceed the calculated boundary rather than equal it. Since $18$ sessions yield exactly $45 + 6(18) = 153 \ge 150$, no additional session is needed.

###### Option 3
Results from dividing $150 \div 6 = 25$ without subtracting the $45$ hours already completed. This targets students who skip the critical step of accounting for the hours already earned through the summer internship before solving for the number of sessions needed.

###### Option 4
Results from adding the hours already completed to the total requirement instead of subtracting ($150 + 45 = 195$), then dividing by the hours per session ($195 \div 6 = 32.5$) and rounding up to $33$. This targets students who set up the problem incorrectly by adding rather than subtracting the completed hours, a common error when translating word problems into mathematical expressions.
