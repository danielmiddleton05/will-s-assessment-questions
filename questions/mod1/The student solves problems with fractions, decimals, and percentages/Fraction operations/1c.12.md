#### Task Description

**Given:** A patient is prescribed a three-day course of oral medication. On the first day, the patient takes $\frac{1}{4}$ of the total prescribed medication. On the second day, the patient takes $\frac{1}{6}$ of the total prescribed medication.

What fraction of the medication remains to be taken on the third day?

###### Option 2 Correct
$\frac{7}{12}$


###### Option 3 Incorrect
$\frac{2}{3}$


###### Option 4 Incorrect

##### Explanation
First, find the total medication taken over the first two days by adding $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6}$. Using a common denominator of $12$: $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{12}$. Adding: $\frac{3}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$. To find what remains, subtract from the whole: $1 - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{12}{12} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Correctly adds the fractions but fails to subtract from the whole — Student finds the common denominator correctly and adds $\frac{3}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$, but stops there without subtracting this amount from $1$ to find what remains.

###### Option 3
Fails to multiply numerator when converting — Student converts $\frac{1}{4}$ to $\frac{2}{12}$ (multiplying only the numerator by 2 instead of 3) and correctly converts $\frac{1}{6}$ to $\frac{2}{12}$. Adding: $\frac{2}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}$. Then subtracts: $1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$.

###### Option 4
Subtracts fractions instead of adding them — Student finds the common denominator correctly but subtracts the Day 2 amount from the Day 1 amount: $\frac{3}{12} - \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{12}$, then calculates the remaining medication as $1 - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.
