**Given:** A statistician uses the formula $\sqrt{a \times b}$ to calculate a composite variability index, where $a$ and $b$ represent variance measures from two independent samples.

##### Explanation
To calculate the composite variability index, first multiply the variance measures: $9 \times 16 = 144$. Then take the square root of the product: $\sqrt{144} = 12$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Targets students who add the values first, then take the square root: $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$. This reflects confusion about the order of operations within the radical expression.

###### Option 3
Targets students who add instead of multiplying and forget to take the square root: $9 + 16 = 25$. This represents a fundamental misunderstanding of both the required operation and the application of the square root.

###### Option 4
Targets students who correctly multiply the values but forget to take the square root: $9 \times 16 = 144$. This reflects completing only the first step of the calculation and reporting the intermediate result as the final answer.
