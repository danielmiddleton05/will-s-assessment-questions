#### Task Description

A warehouse manager calculates that a square storage section has an area of 50 square feet. To order baseboard trim for all four walls, the manager needs to know the length of each side, which equals $\sqrt{50}$ feet.

What correctly explains whether $\sqrt{50}$ is rational or irrational?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Rational, because $\sqrt{50}$ simplifies to $5\sqrt{2}$, which is a product of integers.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Rational, because $\sqrt{50}$ simplifies to $\frac{50}{2}$, which equals the integer 25.


###### Option 3 Correct
Irrational, because $\sqrt{50}$ cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Irrational, because the square root symbol in the expression indicates the result is irrational.

##### Explanation
$\sqrt{50}$ simplifies to $5\sqrt{2}$. Since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational—it cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers—and the product of a nonzero integer and an irrational number is irrational, $\sqrt{50}$ is also irrational. Option 3 correctly identifies this classification and provides valid reasoning. Option 1 correctly simplifies $\sqrt{50}$ to $5\sqrt{2}$ but erroneously calls it "a product of integers," treating $\sqrt{2}$ as though it equals the integer 2. Option 2 confuses the square root operation with dividing by 2, incorrectly claiming that $\sqrt{50} = \frac{50}{2} = 25$. Option 4 arrives at the correct classification but uses flawed reasoning, since square roots of perfect squares (e.g., $\sqrt{4} = 2$, $\sqrt{9} = 3$) are rational.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Misinterprets the simplified form $5\sqrt{2}$ as a product of two integers (5 and 2), concluding the result is rational. Students making this error either confuse $\sqrt{2}$ with 2 or believe that because both 5 and the radicand 2 are integers, the entire expression must be rational. They do not recognize that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational and that multiplying it by an integer preserves its irrationality.

###### Option 2
Confuses the square root operation with dividing by 2, concluding that $\sqrt{50} = \frac{50}{2} = 25$, which is an integer and therefore rational. Students making this error do not understand the definition of a square root—finding a number that, when multiplied by itself, yields the radicand—and instead apply an incorrect arithmetic operation to the radicand.

###### Option 4
Reaches the correct classification (irrational) but relies on the false generalization that the square root symbol itself signals irrationality. This reasoning fails for perfect squares such as $\sqrt{25} = 5$ or $\sqrt{64} = 8$. Students selecting this option associate the radical symbol with irrationality rather than evaluating whether the radicand is a perfect square.
