##### Explanation
$\sqrt{5}$ cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers, making it irrational. The value $0.\overline{6}$ equals $\frac{2}{3}$ (a repeating decimal is rational), $\sqrt{16} = 4$ (a perfect square yields an integer), and $- \sqrt{\frac{9}{4}} = - \frac{3}{2}$ (the square root of a ratio of perfect squares is rational).

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Confuses repeating decimals with non-terminating, non-repeating decimals. Students may think any decimal that continues indefinitely is irrational, not recognizing that repeating patterns indicate rational numbers ($0.\overline{6} = \frac{2}{3}$).

###### Option 2
Assumes all square roots are irrational. Students may not evaluate $\sqrt{16}$ to recognize it equals the integer 4, which is rational. The radical symbol triggers an association with irrationality without checking whether the radicand is a perfect square.

###### Option 4
Assumes square roots of fractions are irrational. Students may not recognize that $- \sqrt{\frac{9}{4}} = - \frac{\sqrt{9}}{\sqrt{4}} = - \frac{3}{2}$, which is a ratio of integers. The combination of a radical and a fraction appears complex but simplifies to a rational number.
