#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital conducted a study on patient recovery outcomes, recording the patient's mobility score improvement after 8 weeks based on the number of weekly physical therapy (PT) sessions. The scatterplot below displays the collected data.

![A scatterplot displays the relationship between the number of weekly physical therapy sessions on the horizontal axis and patient mobility score improvement on the vertical axis. Based on this scatterplot, how does patient mobility score improvement change as the number of weekly PT sessions increases?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
Mobility score improvement tends to increase as weekly PT sessions increase.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Mobility score improvement tends to decrease as weekly PT sessions increase.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Mobility score improvement remains roughly the same regardless of weekly PT sessions.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Mobility score improvement increases initially but then reverses as weekly PT sessions increase.

##### Explanation
When data points trend from the lower left to the upper right of a scatterplot, both variables tend to increase together. This describes a positive relationship: as the number of weekly physical therapy sessions increases, the patient's mobility score improvement also tends to increase. The student must read the pattern in the scatterplot and translate it into a contextual statement rather than simply selecting a directional label.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
Mobility score improvement tends to decrease as weekly PT sessions increase — Reverses the contextual interpretation of the trend direction.
This targets students who misread the upward trend in the scatterplot or confuse which variable is increasing and which is decreasing. A decrease in mobility score as sessions increase would require points trending from upper left to lower right.

###### Option 3
Mobility score improvement remains roughly the same regardless of weekly PT sessions — Misinterprets a clear trend as no relationship.
This targets students who focus on the scatter of individual data points rather than the overall trend. If there were truly no relationship, the points would appear as a random cloud with no discernible upward or downward pattern.

###### Option 4
Mobility score improvement increases initially but then reverses as weekly PT sessions increase — Describes a curvilinear pattern not present in the data.
This targets students who over-interpret minor deviations from a straight line as evidence of a changing direction. The overall trend in the data is consistently upward, not a curve that reverses at some point.
