#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student analyzes relationships between variables in real-world contexts, including correlation and regression.

##### Evidence Statement
The student interprets relationships shown in scatterplots.

##### Aligned Content
Variability (spread around the pattern)

##### Explanation
When data points cluster tightly around a trend line, there is less variability, meaning predictions based on that relationship are more consistent. Scatterplot A's tight clustering indicates that knowing a student's study hours provides a more precise prediction of their test score. Scatterplot B's wide spread indicates more unexplained variation, making predictions less consistent even though both relationships follow the same direction.

##### Domain

Education

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Scatterplot B has a stronger correlation between study hours and test scores than Scatterplot A. — Confuses spread with strength

This reverses the relationship between spread and correlation strength. Wider spread around a trend line indicates weaker correlation, not stronger. Students selecting this option may confuse variability with strength or misunderstand that tight clustering represents a stronger, more predictable relationship.

###### Option 2
The relationship in Scatterplot A is negative while the relationship in Scatterplot B is positive. — Confuses variability with direction

The spread of data points around a trend line does not determine the direction of the relationship. Direction is determined by whether the trend line slopes upward (positive) or downward (negative). The given information states both scatterplots have the same direction, so this option targets students who conflate the concepts of variability and direction.

###### Option 3
Scatterplot B provides more reliable predictions about individual students than Scatterplot A. — Inverts the relationship between spread and reliability

This states the opposite of what is true. Greater spread means less reliability in predictions because there is more unexplained variation. Students selecting this may mistakenly believe that wider spread indicates more information or coverage, when it actually indicates less predictive precision.
