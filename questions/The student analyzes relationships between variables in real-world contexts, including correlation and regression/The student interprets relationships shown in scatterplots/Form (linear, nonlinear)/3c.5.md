#### Task Description

**Given:** A software development team tracked how their mobile application's load time changed as new features were integrated across multiple updates. The scatterplot below displays the results.

![A scatter plot with Number of Integrated Features on the x-axis ranging from 5 to 50 and App Load Time in seconds on the y-axis. Data points show a gradual increase in load time at lower feature counts that becomes increasingly steep beyond around 25 features, forming an upward-curving pattern.](https://k3-production-bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/df702ee9-6add-4c4b-a09e-7f80d6d47a1b_optimized.jpg)

Based on this data, what type of relationship exists between the number of integrated features and the app load time?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The relationship is linear because the data points follow a straight-line pattern with no visible curvature.


###### Option 2 Correct
The relationship is nonlinear because the rate of increase in load time accelerates as more features are added.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The relationship is linear because the increase in load time between 5 and 25 features is the same as the increase between 25 and 50 features.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
There is no relationship because the data points do not follow a clear pattern.

##### Explanation
The scatterplot shows that load time increases slowly when fewer features are present but rises more steeply as the feature count grows. This changing rate of increase produces a curved (nonlinear) pattern. A linear relationship would require a constant rate of change throughout the entire range, but the data clearly show the rate accelerating. Because the data follow a curved trend rather than a straight-line trend, the relationship is nonlinear.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
This distractor appeals to students who do not carefully examine the shape of the data. The scatterplot clearly shows curvature—load time rises gradually at first and then increasingly steeply. Claiming the points follow a straight line with no curvature directly contradicts the visible pattern, making this option clearly incorrect.

###### Option 3
This distractor targets students who do not compare rates of change across the range. A linear relationship would require equal changes in load time over equal intervals of features. However, the scatterplot shows that load time increases much more steeply from 25 to 50 features than from 5 to 25, confirming the relationship is nonlinear. By requiring the student to evaluate whether changes are equal over equal intervals, this option tests interpretation of the pattern's form rather than its direction.

###### Option 4
The data clearly display a systematic pattern—load time increases as the number of features increases. This distractor may appeal to students who focus on the spread of individual data points away from a perfect curve rather than on the overall trend the points collectively form.
