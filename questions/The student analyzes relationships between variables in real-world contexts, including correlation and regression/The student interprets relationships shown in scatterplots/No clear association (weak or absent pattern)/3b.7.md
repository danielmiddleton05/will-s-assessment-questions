#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student analyzes relationships between variables in real-world contexts, including correlation and regression.

##### Evidence Statement
The student interprets relationships shown in scatterplots

##### Aligned Content
No clear association (weak or absent pattern)

##### Explanation
When data points in a scatterplot appear scattered randomly with no discernible pattern or trend, this indicates no clear association (or an absent/very weak association) between the two variables. In this context, knowing a patient's age would not help predict their emergency room wait time. A strong positive association would show points trending upward from left to right. A strong negative association would show points trending downward from left to right. A nonlinear association would show points following a recognizable curved pattern.

##### Domain
Health

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
There is a strong positive association between the two variables — Misinterprets random scatter as an upward trend. Students may select this if they misread the visual description and assume there must be some directional relationship, confuse the concept of "scattered" with points that spread upward, or default to assuming variables are positively related without evaluating the actual pattern described.

###### Option 2
There is a nonlinear association between the two variables — Confuses random scatter with a curved pattern. Students may select this if they think that "no straight-line pattern" automatically means a curve exists, misunderstand that nonlinear associations still require a recognizable predictable pattern, or conflate the absence of a linear trend with the presence of a nonlinear one.

###### Option 4
There is a strong negative association between the two variables — Assumes an inverse relationship must exist. Students may select this if they believe that healthcare variables typically have inverse relationships, misunderstand what "no discernible trend" means in the context of scatterplots, or confuse randomness with a downward pattern.
