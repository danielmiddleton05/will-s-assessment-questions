#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student analyzes relationships between variables in real-world contexts, including correlation and regression.

##### Evidence Statement
The student interprets relationships shown in scatterplots.

##### Aligned Content
Strength of association

##### Explanation
Strength of association is determined by how tightly points cluster around a trend line, regardless of whether the slope is positive or negative. Scatterplot B shows points that are tightly clustered, indicating a strong (negative) association. Scatterplot A shows points that are widely scattered, indicating a weaker (positive) association.

##### Domain
Business

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Contradicts the described patterns. Both scatterplots show clear directional trends (one upward, one downward). This distractor identifies candidates who believe an association only exists if it is positive or who fail to recognize any pattern in scattered data.

###### Option 2
Misinterprets the visual evidence. Candidates might choose this because the trend is positive (upward), incorrectly assuming positive correlations are "stronger" than negative ones, or they may fail to notice the degree of dispersion (clustering) relative to the trend line.

###### Option 4
Ignores the difference in clustering patterns. While both show a clear direction, they differ significantly in consistency. This distractor identifies candidates who can identify the presence of a trend but cannot distinguish between degrees of correlation strength.
