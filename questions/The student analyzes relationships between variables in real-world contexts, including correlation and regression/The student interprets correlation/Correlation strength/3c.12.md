#### Task Description

An education researcher reports the following Pearson correlation coefficients from a study of student performance:

| Variables | $r$ |
|---|---|
| Weekly tutoring hours and number of incorrect exam answers | $-0.82$ |
| Daily reading minutes and reading comprehension score | $0.74$ |
| Attendance rate and course grade | $0.69$ |
| Class size and average student test score | $-0.47$ |

Which pair of variables has the strongest linear relationship?

##### Explanation
The strength of a linear relationship is determined by the absolute value of the Pearson correlation coefficient, regardless of whether the correlation is positive or negative. Comparing absolute values: $|-0.82| = 0.82$, $|0.74| = 0.74$, $|0.69| = 0.69$, and $|-0.47| = 0.47$. Because $0.82$ is the largest absolute value, the relationship between weekly tutoring hours and number of incorrect exam answers is the strongest linear relationship among the four pairs. The negative sign indicates only that the variables move in opposite directions—more tutoring is associated with fewer incorrect answers—but it does not diminish the strength.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
Students may select daily reading minutes and reading comprehension score ($r = 0.74$) because it is the highest positive value. This error reflects a misconception that positive correlations are inherently stronger than negative correlations, or that the negative sign reduces the strength of a relationship. In reality, strength is determined by absolute value alone.

###### Option 3
Students may select attendance rate and course grade ($r = 0.69$) if they focus on the intuitive appeal of the relationship (attendance leading to better grades) rather than comparing the magnitudes of the coefficients. This distractor targets the tendency to let the perceived logic of a relationship override mathematical comparison.

###### Option 4
Students may select class size and average student test score ($r = -0.47$) if they confuse the negative sign with indicating a stronger effect, or if they misread the table. This represents a moderate correlation and tests whether students can distinguish moderate from strong relationships when both share the same direction.
