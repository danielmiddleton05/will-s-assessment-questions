#### Task Description

A hospital administrator reviews a clinical study comparing two drugs for treating chronic pain. Overall, Drug A has a 72% effectiveness rate and Drug B has a 64% effectiveness rate. When the results are separated by condition severity, however, Drug B has a higher effectiveness rate than Drug A among patients with mild conditions and also among patients with severe conditions. Further review reveals that Drug A was prescribed predominantly to patients with mild conditions, who tend to respond well to any treatment.

What statistical concept explains the reversal between the overall and subgroup-level effectiveness rates?

##### Explanation
Simpson's paradox occurs when a trend that appears in combined data reverses when the data is separated into subgroups. Here, Drug A has a higher overall effectiveness rate (72% vs. 64%), but Drug B outperforms Drug A in both the mild and severe condition subgroups. Because Drug A was prescribed predominantly to patients with mild conditions—who respond well to any treatment—Drug A's overall rate is inflated by the disproportionate representation of these easier-to-treat patients. The unequal distribution of patients across severity levels creates a weighted average that reverses the subgroup-level trend, which is the defining characteristic of Simpson's paradox.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Survivorship bias occurs when analysis focuses only on subjects that survived or completed a process, ignoring those who dropped out or were excluded. A student might suspect that only successful patient outcomes were recorded, making this option seem plausible. However, survivorship bias does not explain why a trend systematically reverses between subgroup and aggregate levels. The reversal here is a mathematical consequence of unequal group sizes across severity categories, not selective inclusion of outcomes.

###### Option 2
Regression to the mean describes the tendency for extreme measurements to move closer to the average upon subsequent measurement. A student might confuse the apparent shift in effectiveness rates with a natural regression effect. However, regression to the mean applies to repeated measurements of the same subjects over time, not to a systematic reversal between subgroup and aggregate data within a single study. The pattern described is driven by the structure of weighted averages, not by temporal fluctuation.

###### Option 3
Sampling error refers to differences between a sample statistic and the true population parameter that arise from random chance. A student might attribute the contradictory findings to an unreliable or poorly drawn sample. However, the pattern described is a consistent and systematic reversal driven by the mathematical structure of weighted averages across unequal group sizes, not by random fluctuation in the data. Sampling error would not reliably produce higher subgroup rates for one drug while simultaneously producing a lower aggregate rate.
