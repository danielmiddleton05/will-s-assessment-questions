Effects of outliers on correlation

##### Explanation
The correlation coefficient ($r$) measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship. When a dataset has a near-perfect correlation ($r = 0.999$), the points lie almost exactly on a straight line. An outlier that is "off-trend"—meaning it does not follow the linear pattern established by the rest of the data—will pull the correlation coefficient closer to zero. Even in a sample of 200, a distant outlier increases the sum of squared deviations from the regression line, thereby weakening the $r$ value.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
This reflects the misconception that higher y-values or "more data" in a positive trend automatically increase the correlation. However, $r$ measures the consistency of the linear pattern, not the magnitude of the values.

###### Option 3
This is incorrect because $r$ is highly sensitive to outliers. While a larger sample size ($n=200$) provides some stability, a "distant" outlier (especially one significantly far from the trend line) will still mathematically lower the correlation coefficient.

###### Option 4
This option focuses on the classification of the resulting correlation rather than the *effect* of the outlier. While the $r$ value may remain numerically high, the addition of an off-trend point necessarily decreases the strength of the relationship compared to the original 0.999.
