#### Task Description

**Given:** A teacher explains that the theoretical probability of rolling a 4 on a standard die is $\frac{1}{6}$. A student then rolls the die 60 times and gets a 4 exactly 8 times.

What is the relationship between the theoretical probability ($\frac{1}{6}$) and the empirical result ($\frac{8}{60}$)?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The theoretical probability is incorrect because it does not match the observed result.


###### Option 2 Correct
Both values are valid, with the empirical result approximating the theoretical probability.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The empirical result is invalid because it differs from the theoretical probability.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The two values cannot be compared because they measure different phenomena.

##### Aligned Content
Relationship between theoretical and empirical

##### Explanation
Theoretical probability represents the expected long-run proportion under ideal conditions, while empirical probability reflects actual observations. The empirical result ($\frac{8}{60} \approx 0.133$) reasonably approximates the theoretical probability ($\frac{1}{6} \approx 0.167$). Small differences are expected due to natural variation in finite samples. A statistician must understand that these two approaches measure the same underlying likelihood through different methods and that empirical results will vary around theoretical predictions.

##### Domain

Education

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Students may select this if they expect theoretical predictions to match exactly with any observed results, not understanding that natural variation causes differences in finite samples. This reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of theoretical probability as a long-run expectation rather than a predictor of exact outcomes.

###### Option 3
Students may select this if they believe observations must exactly match theory to be considered valid or meaningful. This reflects a misconception that empirical data is only useful when it precisely confirms theoretical predictions, rather than understanding that variation is expected and informative.

###### Option 4
Students may select this if they overstate the distinction between theoretical and empirical probability, failing to recognize that both approaches aim to quantify the same underlying likelihood. This reflects confusion about how different probability methods relate to one another and to the phenomena they describe.
