#### Task Description

**Given:** A school's reading program assessed 500 third-grade students at the end of the year. The results showed 120 students scored "below grade level," 215 students scored "at grade level," and 165 students scored "above grade level."

Based on these results, how do the likelihoods of the different scoring outcomes compare for a randomly selected student from this group?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Scoring below grade level is more likely than scoring above grade level.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Scoring above grade level is equally likely as scoring at grade level.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Scoring at grade level is less likely than scoring above grade level.


###### Option 4 Correct
Scoring above grade level is more likely than scoring below grade level.

##### Explanation
The student must evaluate each comparative statement against the observed frequencies. "Above grade level" (165 students) has a higher frequency than "below grade level" (120 students), so scoring above grade level is more likely than scoring below grade level. Events with higher observed frequencies have higher empirical probability, making Option 4 the only statement that correctly compares the likelihoods. Unlike simply identifying a maximum, this question requires parsing relational language and verifying specific pairwise comparisons.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Scoring below grade level is more likely than scoring above grade level — Reverses the correct comparison between below (120) and above (165). This targets students who misread the data values for each category or who confuse "below" and "above" when matching labels to numbers.

###### Option 2
Scoring above grade level is equally likely as scoring at grade level — Incorrectly treats 165 and 215 as equivalent. This targets students who assume outcomes near the middle of a distribution should be roughly equal, or who do not carefully compare the two different frequencies.

###### Option 3
Scoring at grade level is less likely than scoring above grade level — Reverses the relationship between at grade level (215) and above grade level (165). This targets students who misidentify which of two close-sounding categories has the higher count, or who confuse the direction of a "less likely" comparison.
