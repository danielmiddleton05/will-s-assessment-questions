#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital infection control report shows that 18 out of 120 surgical patients in Building A developed a post-surgical infection, and 9 out of 30 surgical patients in Building B developed a post-surgical infection. A hospital administrator claims that Building A poses a greater infection risk because more patients were infected there than in Building B.

How does comparing the proportion of infected patients in each building address this claim?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The proportions confirm the claim because Building A has twice as many total infections as Building B.


###### Option 2 Correct
The proportions contradict the claim because Building B's infection rate of 30% is higher than Building A's rate of 15%.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The proportions show that both buildings have an equal infection risk, so the claim overstates the difference.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The proportions confirm the claim because Building A's larger patient population increases the overall likelihood of infection.

##### Explanation
The administrator's claim is based on absolute counts (18 > 9), but probability is determined by proportion relative to the total. Building A has an infection rate of $\frac{18}{120} = 0.15$ (15%), while Building B has an infection rate of $\frac{9}{30} = 0.30$ (30%). Since $0.30 > 0.15$, Building B actually has the higher infection rate, which contradicts the administrator's claim. A randomly selected surgical patient in Building B is twice as likely to have developed an infection compared to one in Building A.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The proportions confirm the claim because Building A has twice as many total infections as Building B — Targets students who equate a higher absolute count of infections (18 > 9) with a higher rate of infection, essentially restating the administrator's flawed reasoning in different terms without actually computing or comparing proportions.

###### Option 3
The proportions show that both buildings have an equal infection risk, so the claim overstates the difference — Targets students who recognize that proportion rather than raw count determines probability but fail to accurately calculate or compare the specific rates (15% vs. 30%), incorrectly concluding the rates are equivalent.

###### Option 4
The proportions confirm the claim because Building A's larger patient population increases the overall likelihood of infection — Targets students who confuse sample size with probability, incorrectly reasoning that a larger total number of patients (120 vs. 30) inherently produces a greater likelihood of infection for any individual patient in that group.
