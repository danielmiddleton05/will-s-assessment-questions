#### Task Description

**Given:** A community health clinic recorded blood pressure (BP) readings for 400 patients during a screening event, where 60 patients had readings classified as "high," 100 patients had readings classified as "elevated," and the remaining patients had readings classified as "normal."

Based on this data, what is the estimated probability that a randomly selected patient from this screening would have a "normal" BP reading?

##### Explanation
First, determine how many patients had "normal" readings by subtracting the other two categories from the total:
$400 - 60 - 100 = 240$ patients with "normal" readings.

Then, calculate the relative frequency to estimate the probability:
$240 \div 400 = 0.60$

The estimated probability that a randomly selected patient would have a "normal" BP reading is $0.60$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
0.15 — This distractor targets students who use the "high" BP count (60) directly in the numerator instead of first calculating the number of "normal" readings, computing $60 \div 400 = 0.15$. These students select data for the wrong category rather than performing the necessary subtraction step.

###### Option 2
0.25 — This distractor targets students who use the "elevated" BP count (100) directly in the numerator instead of calculating the "normal" count, computing $100 \div 400 = 0.25$. Similar to Option 1, this reflects confusion over which category the question asks about.

###### Option 4
0.75 — This distractor targets students who recognize that subtraction is needed to find the "normal" count but subtract only one of the two non-normal categories from the total. For example, computing $400 - 100 = 300$, then $300 \div 400 = 0.75$, forgetting to also remove the 60 "high" readings.
