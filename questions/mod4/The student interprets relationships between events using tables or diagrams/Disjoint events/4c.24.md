#### Task Description

**Given:** A financial services firm reviewed 250 client accounts to analyze patterns in service usage. The two-way frequency table below categorizes each account by whether it received Tax Advisory services and whether it received Audit Services during the past fiscal year.

| | Audit Services: Yes | Audit Services: No | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tax Advisory: Yes** | 0 | 130 | 130 |
| **Tax Advisory: No** | 75 | 45 | 120 |
| **Total** | 75 | 175 | 250 |

Based on the data in this table, how should the events "account receives Tax Advisory" and "account receives Audit Services" be described in probability terms?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Complementary, because every client account falls into at least one of the two service categories


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Independent, because each service is managed by a separate department within the firm


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Dependent, because selecting one service increases the likelihood of selecting the other


###### Option 4 Correct
Mutually exclusive, because no client account received both services during the same fiscal year

##### Explanation
Mutually exclusive (disjoint) events are defined by the condition P(A and B) = 0, meaning the two events cannot co-occur. The two-way frequency table shows that the joint cell—where both Tax Advisory and Audit Services are "Yes"—contains a count of 0 out of 250 accounts. This means no client account received both services in the same fiscal year, which is the defining characteristic of mutually exclusive events. Unlike the visual recognition required with a Venn diagram, this question requires the student to read a frequency table, identify the critical zero in the joint cell, and connect that numerical pattern to the correct probability term. The events are not complementary because 45 accounts received neither service, meaning the two events do not exhaust all possibilities.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Complementary, because every client account falls into at least one of the two service categories — Targets students who confuse mutual exclusivity with complementary (exhaustive) events. While the table does show a zero in the joint cell, 45 accounts received neither Tax Advisory nor Audit Services. This means the two events do not account for all possible outcomes, which is required for events to be complementary. A student who notices the non-overlap but fails to check the "neither" cell (Tax Advisory: No, Audit Services: No = 45) may incorrectly assume the events are complementary.

###### Option 2
Independent, because each service is managed by a separate department within the firm — Targets students who confuse operational separation with statistical independence. Independence requires P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Here, P(A) × P(B) = (130/250) × (75/250) = 0.156, while P(A and B) = 0/250 = 0. Since 0 ≠ 0.156, the events are not independent. Students who equate "separate departments" or "different categories" with statistical independence may select this option.

###### Option 3
Dependent, because selecting one service increases the likelihood of selecting the other — Targets students who recognize that the events are statistically related but mischaracterize the nature of that relationship. While mutually exclusive events with non-zero probabilities are technically dependent (knowing one occurred changes the probability of the other), the direction described here is backward: receiving one service reduces the probability of the other to zero rather than increasing it. Students who partially understand dependency but do not distinguish it from mutual exclusivity may find this appealing.
