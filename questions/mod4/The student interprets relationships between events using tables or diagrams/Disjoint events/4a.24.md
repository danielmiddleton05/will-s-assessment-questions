#### Task Description

**Given:** A university enrollment system includes several student classifications. Full-time and part-time are two of several possible classifications in the system. The system does not allow any student to be classified as both full-time and part-time simultaneously, as shown in the visualization below.

In probability terminology, which term best describes the relationship between the events
“student is Full-time” and “student is Part-time”?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Complementary, because together they include all students in the enrollment system.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Independent, because the classifications are separate categories.


###### Option 3 Correct
Mutually exclusive, because a student cannot belong to both classifications at the same time.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Overlapping, because some students may qualify for both categories.

##### Explanation
Mutually exclusive (also called disjoint) events cannot occur simultaneously. Since a student cannot be classified as both full-time and part-time at the same time, these events are mutually exclusive—if one occurs, the other cannot occur for that same student. The given statement specifically emphasizes that the system "does not allow any student to be classified as both," which directly describes the defining characteristic of mutually exclusive events. By specifying that these are just two of various classifications, the system ensures the events are not necessarily complementary (exhaustive).

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Complementary, because the two categories together include all students in the enrollment system — Confuses mutual exclusivity with exhaustive/complementary events. This targets students who recognize that the categories might be exhaustive but don't distinguish this from mutual exclusivity. While events can be both mutually exclusive and complementary, the question focuses on the fact that events cannot co-occur, which specifically defines mutual exclusivity. Complementary is a stronger condition requiring both mutual exclusivity and exhaustiveness, which is explicitly contradicted by the stem mentioning "various" classifications.

###### Option 2
Independent, because full-time and part-time are separate classifications in the system — Confuses mutual exclusivity with independence. This targets students who confuse whether events can co-occur (mutual exclusivity) with whether one event's occurrence affects another's probability (independence). The phrase "separate classifications" may seem to suggest independence, but being unable to occur simultaneously is the definition of mutual exclusivity, not independence.

###### Option 4
Dependent, because a student's classification status in one category affects their classification in the other — Targets students who recognize that the events are related because they cannot co-occur. While non-zero mutually exclusive events are technically dependent (as the occurrence of one changes the probability of the other to zero), "mutually exclusive" is the standard and more precise term for events that are defined specifically by their inability to occur simultaneously.
