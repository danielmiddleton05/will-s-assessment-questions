#### Task Description

**Given:** A school district created the following frequency table showing the distribution of 500 students by grade level and extracurricular participation:

| | Participates | Does Not Participate |
|---|---|---|
| Elementary (K–5) | 80 | 120 |
| Middle School (6–8) | 170 | 130 |

What statement about probability is supported by this frequency table?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
A randomly selected student from the entire group is equally likely to participate as not participate in extracurriculars.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
An elementary student selected at random is more likely to participate than not participate in extracurriculars.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
A middle school student selected at random is equally likely to participate as not participate in extracurriculars.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Randomly selecting an elementary non-participant is more likely than selecting a middle school participant.

##### Explanation
To evaluate the statements, determine row totals, column totals, and the overall total from the two-way frequency table:
- Elementary total: $80 + 120 = 200$
- Middle School total: $170 + 130 = 300$
- Participates total: $80 + 170 = 250$
- Does Not Participate total: $120 + 130 = 250$
- Overall total: $500$

Across all students, 250 out of 500 participate ($0.50$ probability) and 250 out of 500 do not ($0.50$ probability). Since $250 = 250$, participation and non-participation are equally likely overall.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
An elementary student selected at random is more likely to participate than not participate in extracurriculars — This targets students who misread the table values within the elementary row or who assume participation must be higher for all groups without checking the frequencies.

###### Option 3
A middle school student selected at random is equally likely to participate as not participate in extracurriculars — This targets students who see the middle school numbers (170 and 130) and perceive them as close enough to be equal, or who confuse the overall equal column totals with within-row equality.

###### Option 4
Randomly selecting an elementary non-participant is more likely than selecting a middle school participant — This targets students who focus on the elementary non-participant value (120) being relatively large without comparing it to the middle school participant value (170), or who confuse group-level rates with overall joint frequencies.
