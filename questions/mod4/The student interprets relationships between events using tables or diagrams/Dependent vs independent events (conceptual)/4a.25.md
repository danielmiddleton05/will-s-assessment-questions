#### Task Description

**Given:** A quality control department tests computer chips using two inspections. Inspection A checks for surface defects, and Inspection B checks for processing errors. Data analysis reveals the following results, which are visualized in the chart below:
- 5% of all chips fail Inspection A
- Among chips that fail Inspection A, 30% also fail Inspection B
- Among chips that pass Inspection A, only 8% fail Inspection B

![Bar chart titled "Conditional Probability of Failing Inspection B". The x-axis represents the "Inspection A Result" with categories "Fail A" and "Pass A". The y-axis represents the "Percent Failing Inspection B". What does this information indicate about the relationship between the two inspection results?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The inspection results are independent because they measure different types of defects


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The inspection results are independent because the probability of failing Inspection B is constant regardless of the Inspection A result


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The inspection results are dependent because both inspections are performed on the same sample of computer chips


###### Option 4 Correct
The inspection results are dependent because failing Inspection A changes the probability of failing Inspection B

##### Explanation
Two events are statistically dependent when the occurrence of one event changes the probability of the other. Here, the probability of failing Inspection B is 30% when Inspection A is failed, but only 8% when Inspection A is passed. Since these probabilities differ, the inspection outcomes are dependent.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The inspection results are independent because they measure different types of defects — Incorrectly equates different measurements with independence

This targets students who assume that measuring different things automatically means independence. Statistical independence is determined by whether probabilities change, not by what is being measured.

###### Option 2
The inspection results are independent because the probability of failing Inspection B is constant regardless of the Inspection A result — This correctly defines a condition for independence but misinterprets the provided data. The chart and text explicitly show the failure rate for B changes from 30% to 8% depending on the result of A.

###### Option 3
The inspection results are dependent because both inspections are performed on the same sample of computer chips — Targets the misconception that events are dependent simply because they occur on the same subject or within the same trial. Statistical dependency requires an actual change in conditional probability, not just a shared subject.
