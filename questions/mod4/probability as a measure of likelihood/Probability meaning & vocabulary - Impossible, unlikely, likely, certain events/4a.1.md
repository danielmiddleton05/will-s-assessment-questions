##### Explanation
When an event occurs every time without exception, its probability is 1, which is described as "certain." The scenario states hand hygiene is performed before every patient contact, making this outcome certain when selecting any patient contact.

##### Distractors

###### Option 3
Likely — Confuses high probability with certainty
This targets students who recognize the positive nature of the statement but don't distinguish between events that happen frequently (likely, probability less than 1) and events that happen without exception (certain, probability equals 1).

###### Option 2
Unlikely — Reverses the interpretation
This targets students who may misread the scenario or confuse probability vocabulary, selecting the opposite of what the data indicates.

###### Option 1
Impossible — Confuses opposite ends of the probability scale
This targets students who confuse "impossible" (never happens, probability 0) with "certain" (always happens, probability 1), mixing up the two extreme endpoints of probability.
