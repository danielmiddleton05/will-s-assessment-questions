#### Task Description

A school district tracked the reading levels of 200 third-grade students at the end of the school year. Of these students, 52 were reading above grade level, 88 were reading at grade level, and the rest were reading below grade level.

What is the probability that a randomly selected student from this group is reading at or above grade level, expressed on the standard probability scale?

##### Explanation
To find the probability that a randomly selected student is reading at or above grade level, first combine the two relevant groups: 52 (above grade level) + 88 (at grade level) = 140 students. Then express this as a probability on the 0–1 scale by dividing by the total number of students: 140 ÷ 200 = 0.70. This value falls within the valid probability range of 0 to 1 and correctly represents the likelihood of the described event.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
0.26 — Considers only "above grade level" students (52 ÷ 200 = 0.26)

This targets students who misinterpret "at or above grade level" as only "above grade level," failing to include the 88 students who are reading at grade level.

###### Option 2
0.30 — Calculates the complement category (below grade level: 60 ÷ 200 = 0.30)

This targets students who determine the remaining group (200 − 52 − 88 = 60) and mistakenly report the probability of the opposite event (reading below grade level) rather than the event described in the question.
