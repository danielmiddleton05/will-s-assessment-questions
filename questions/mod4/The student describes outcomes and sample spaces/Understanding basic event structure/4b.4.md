##### Explanation
In probability, an event is a specific outcome or collection of outcomes that we are interested in studying. "Daily sales exceed \$10,000" describes a particular condition being satisfied, making it an event. The sample space would be all possible sales amounts, the probability would be $\frac{12}{30}$, and each day represents a trial.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
The sample space, which is the set of all possible outcomes — Confuses event with sample space

This targets students who don't distinguish between the full set of possibilities (sample space) and a specific subset of interest (event). The sample space would include all possible daily sales figures.

###### Option 3
A trial, which is a single observation or experiment — Confuses event with trial

This targets students who mix up the experiment being conducted (each day is a trial) with the outcome being measured (whether sales exceeded \$10,000).

###### Option 4
A probability, which is a numerical measure of likelihood — Confuses event with its probability

This targets students who conflate the description of a condition with its numerical likelihood. The probability would be $\frac{12}{30}$, but the condition itself is the event.
