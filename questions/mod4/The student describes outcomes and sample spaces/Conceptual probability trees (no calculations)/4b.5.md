##### Explanation
A probability tree diagram systematically shows all possible sequences of outcomes by branching at each decision point. Its purpose is to organize and visualize the complete set of possible paths (the sample space), not to record historical frequency, assume equal likelihood, or predict the most probable path without additional data.

##### Domain

Business

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
To show the actual frequency of each customer decision — Misunderstands the difference between a theoretical model of possibilities and empirical data collected from actual events.

###### Option 2
To prove that all sequences of outcomes are equally likely — Confuses the visual structure of a tree diagram with the mathematical probability of each branch; symmetry in a diagram does not imply equal probability.

###### Option 4
To identify which path is most likely to be chosen by customers — Incorrectly assumes that mapping out all possible sequences is sufficient for predicting which specific outcome is the most probable.
