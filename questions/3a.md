##### Competency
The student analyzes relationships between variables in real-world contexts, including correlation and regression.

Clusters, patterns, deviations

##### Explanation
A pattern in a scatterplot is a consistent, identifiable form that the data follows, revealing a clear relationship between the two variables. In this case, the data points follow a positive linear trend — as temperature rises, cold beverage sales rise in a predictable way. This systematic arrangement of data points is what defines a pattern in a scatterplot, as opposed to a cluster (a distinct grouping of points concentrated in one region, separated from other groups), a deviation (a point or group of points that departs from the overall trend), or a uniform distribution (a term from probability describing a single variable where all values are equally likely).

The question asks about the overall arrangement of the data, which consistently follows a trend rather than departing from one. This distractor targets students who confuse individual variation around a trend with the trend itself.

###### Option 4
A uniform distribution — A uniform distribution is a term from probability that describes a single variable where all values are equally likely, producing a flat spread. It does not describe a directional relationship between two variables on a scatterplot. This distractor targets students who misapply distributional terminology.
