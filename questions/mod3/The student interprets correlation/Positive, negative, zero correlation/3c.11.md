#### Task Description

Three healthcare research findings are described below:

- **Scenario 1:** A clinic found that as patients increase their daily sugar intake, their fasting blood glucose levels tend to rise.
- **Scenario 2:** A rehabilitation center observed that as the number of physical therapy sessions patients complete increases, their self-reported pain levels tend to decrease.
- **Scenario 3:** A hospital study found no consistent pattern between patients' eye color and the length of time they remained hospitalized after knee replacement surgery.

Which option correctly matches each scenario to the type of correlation it demonstrates?



#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
Scenario 1 – Positive, Scenario 2 – Negative, Scenario 3 – Zero


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Scenario 1 – Negative, Scenario 2 – Positive, Scenario 3 – Zero


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Scenario 1 – Positive, Scenario 2 – Zero, Scenario 3 – Negative


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Scenario 1 – Zero, Scenario 2 – Negative, Scenario 3 – Positive

##### Explanation
In Scenario 1, both sugar intake and blood glucose move upward together, which defines a positive correlation. In Scenario 2, as physical therapy sessions increase, pain levels decrease—the variables move in opposite directions, which defines a negative correlation. In Scenario 3, eye color and hospital stay length show no consistent pattern, which defines a zero correlation. Therefore, the correct matching is Scenario 1 – Positive, Scenario 2 – Negative, Scenario 3 – Zero.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
This distractor swaps the labels for Scenarios 1 and 2, assigning negative to the scenario where both variables increase together and positive to the scenario where the variables move in opposite directions. It targets students who confuse the direction associated with positive and negative correlations—those who may mistakenly think "positive" refers to a favorable health outcome (pain decreasing) rather than the direction of the relationship between variables.

###### Option 3
This distractor correctly identifies Scenario 1 as positive but swaps the labels for Scenarios 2 and 3. It assigns zero correlation to the physical therapy scenario and negative correlation to the eye color scenario. It targets students who may partially understand positive correlation but struggle to distinguish between a negative correlation (an inverse pattern) and zero correlation (no pattern at all), particularly when one scenario involves a decrease.

###### Option 4
This distractor assigns zero correlation to Scenario 1, where both variables clearly rise together, and positive correlation to Scenario 3, where no pattern exists. It targets students who may misinterpret "no consistent pattern" as meaning the variables have not yet been proven unrelated, or who confuse situations where both variables are simply present with a positive correlation, regardless of whether they actually co-vary.
