#### Task Description

**Given:** An education researcher finds a Pearson correlation coefficient of $r = 0.45$ between study hours and test scores for 50 students, as displayed in the scatter plot below. One student who studied only 2 hours scored exceptionally high—far above what would be predicted. When this student is removed from the analysis, the Pearson correlation coefficient increases to $r = 0.68$.

![Scatter plot titled 'Study Hours vs. Test Scores' showing a general positive trend in blue data points, with one distinct red outlier point located at 2 study hours and a score of 95, significantly deviating from the main cluster.](https://k3-production-bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/ebc6a211-108d-48b1-b5c7-3d7562505eb4_optimized.jpg)

What does this change demonstrate about outliers?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Outliers strengthen correlation coefficients.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Outliers have no effect on correlation coefficients.


###### Option 3 Correct
Outliers can weaken correlation coefficients.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Outliers change the direction but not the strength of correlations.

Effects of outliers on correlation

##### Explanation
The outlier in this case was inconsistent with the overall positive trend (low study hours but high score). Such outliers pull the correlation toward zero, weakening it. Removing the outlier revealed a stronger underlying relationship, as evidenced by the increase from $r = 0.45$ to $r = 0.68$.

##### Domain
Education

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This represents the misconception that outliers make relationships more extreme or stronger. However, the effect of an outlier depends on its position relative to the trend. Outliers that are inconsistent with the overall trend (as in this example) weaken correlations, while those aligned with but extreme to the trend can strengthen them.

###### Option 2
Directly contradicted by the example. The change from $r = 0.45$ to $r = 0.68$ upon removing one data point clearly demonstrates that outliers can substantially affect correlation values. This distractor appeals to students who may not understand how correlation coefficients are calculated or influenced.

###### Option 4
Incorrectly limits the scope of outlier effects. This example shows the correlation direction remained positive (both 0.45 and 0.68) while the strength changed substantially. Outliers primarily affect the strength of correlations, though extreme cases positioned in opposing quadrants could affect direction as well.
