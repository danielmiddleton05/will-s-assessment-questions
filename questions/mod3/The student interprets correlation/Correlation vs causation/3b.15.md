#### Task Description

**Given:** A technology news article reports: "Study finds strong correlation between time spent on social media and feelings of anxiety among teenagers. Social media use causes teen anxiety."

What is problematic about the article's conclusion?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The conclusion would be correct if the article stated that anxiety causes social media use.


###### Option 2 Correct
Correlation does not establish that one variable causes another.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The article correctly concludes that a strong correlation proves causation.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The correlation should have been negative instead of positive.

Correlation vs causation

##### Explanation
Correlation measures association, not causation. Even with a strong correlation, alternative explanations exist: anxiety might cause increased social media use, or a third variable (like social isolation) might cause both. The article's causal claim goes beyond what correlation can establish.

##### Domain
Technology

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The conclusion would be correct if the article stated that anxiety causes social media use — Focuses on reverse causation
This distractor is plausible because reverse causation is a common alternative explanation for correlational findings. However, it is incorrect because correlational data cannot be used to establish a causal relationship in either direction; simply reversing the claim does not make it statistically sound.

###### Option 3
The article correctly concludes that a strong correlation proves causation — States the exact misconception being tested
This is the erroneous reasoning the article uses. Candidates who share this misconception will select this option because they believe the article's logic is sound, making it an effective distractor for identifying this common mistake in statistical interpretation.

###### Option 4
The correlation should have been negative instead of positive — Introduces an irrelevant critique
The direction of correlation is not the issue. The problem is inferring causation from any correlation, regardless of direction. This distractor shifts focus away from the actual logical error to a non-existent methodological concern.
