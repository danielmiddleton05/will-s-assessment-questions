##### Explanation
Correlation does not imply causation. Both ice cream sales and drowning incidents are likely influenced by warm weather and summer months (a confounding variable), which explains their association without either directly causing the other. A statistician must recognize that observing a correlation between two variables does not establish that one causes the other; instead, both may be influenced by a third variable not included in the analysis.

##### Domain
Health

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This distractor incorrectly assumes that a high correlation implies a direct causal relationship. It represents the common fallacy of mistaking a statistical association for a direct influence between two variables, ignoring the role of potential confounders.

###### Option 3
This distractor misinterprets a synchronized trend as a direct dependency. It incorrectly suggests that because two variables move in the same direction, they must be functionally related, failing to distinguish between a shared pattern and a logical link.

###### Option 4
This distractor conflates the mathematical utility of prediction with an explanation of the relationship's nature. While correlation allows for predictive modeling, it does not justify a conclusion about how or why the variables are connected in a real-world context.
