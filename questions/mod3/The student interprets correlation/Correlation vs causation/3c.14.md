#### Task Description

**Given:** An analyst reviews data from 60 retail stores. Across all stores, the correlation between the average number of employees scheduled per shift and average hourly sales is **0.83**. When the analyst computes the correlation separately within each of three floor-space categories (small, medium, large), the correlation within each category is about **0.04**.  
How should this pattern be interpreted?



#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Interpret it as evidence that increasing employees scheduled per shift will increase hourly sales within each floor-space category.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Interpret it as evidence that higher hourly sales cause managers to schedule more employees per shift within each floor-space category.


###### Option 3 Correct
Interpret it as evidence that floor space is influencing both employees scheduled per shift and hourly sales, creating the overall correlation.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Interpret it as evidence that employees scheduled per shift can be used to reliably forecast hourly sales within each floor-space category.

##### Explanation
The strong overall correlation (0.83) suggests that stores with more employees scheduled per shift also tend to have higher hourly sales when all stores are pooled together. However, the correlation almost disappears (≈ 0.04) when comparing stores within the same floor-space category. This indicates that a third factor—store size/floor space—is likely driving both staffing levels and sales, making the pooled correlation misleading as evidence of a causal staffing effect.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This distractor incorrectly treats the overall correlation as proof that increasing staffing will increase sales, and it ignores that within similarly sized stores the relationship is near zero.

###### Option 2
This distractor assumes a causal direction (sales → staffing) based on correlation. The information provided does not establish direction, and the near-zero within-category correlation undermines a causal interpretation within store-size groups.

###### Option 4
This distractor conflates an overall correlation (driven by differences in store size) with predictive usefulness within each size category. Because the within-category correlation is near zero, staffing is not a reliable predictor among stores of similar size.
