##### Explanation
The slope ($b_1$) of a regression line represents the predicted change in the dependent variable ($y$) for every one-unit increase in the independent variable ($x$). In this study, the independent variable is "steps in thousands" and the dependent variable is "calories burned." Therefore, the slope represents how many additional calories are predicted to be burned for each additional unit (1,000 steps) taken.

##### Domain

Health

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
The predicted total daily calories burned for a patient who takes zero steps — This describes the **y-intercept** ($b_0$) of the regression line, which represents the value of $y$ when $x = 0$. In this context, this would be the predicted basal metabolic rate (calories burned at rest).

###### Option 2
The actual number of calories burned by an individual patient at a specific step count — This is a misconception about regression. A regression line predicts the *average* or *expected* value for a group; individual data points vary and rarely fall exactly on the line.

###### Option 4
The average number of steps taken per day by all patients in the study — This describes the **mean of the independent variable** ($\bar{x}$). While the regression line typically passes through the point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$, the mean itself is a descriptive statistic of the data distribution, not the rate of change (slope) between the two variables.
