#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital administrator conducts a regression analysis to examine the relationship between the number of nurses per shift (explanatory variable) and patient satisfaction scores on a 100-point scale (response variable). The analysis yields a slope of 0.8.

What does this slope value represent in this context?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
Each additional nurse per shift is associated with an average increase of 0.8 points in patient satisfaction scores.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The correlation between nursing staff levels and patient satisfaction is 0.8, indicating a strong positive relationship.


###### Option 3 Incorrect
Exactly 80% of the variation in patient satisfaction scores can be explained by the number of nurses per shift.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Patient satisfaction scores are 0.8 times the number of nurses working per shift.

##### Explanation
The slope in regression represents the average change in the response variable for each one-unit increase in the explanatory variable. A slope of 0.8 means that for each additional nurse per shift, patient satisfaction scores increase by 0.8 points on average. Option 2 confuses slope with correlation coefficient, which measures strength and direction of a linear relationship but is a different statistic. Option 3 confuses slope with the coefficient of determination (R²), which measures explained variance. Option 4 incorrectly treats the relationship as purely multiplicative, ignoring that regression models include an intercept and represent average predicted change, not exact multiplication.

##### Distractors
###### Option 2
The correlation between nursing staff levels and patient satisfaction is 0.8, indicating a strong positive relationship — Confuses slope with correlation coefficient. This targets students who conflate different regression statistics. While both describe aspects of a linear relationship, correlation (r) measures strength and direction on a -1 to 1 scale, whereas slope measures the rate of change in the response variable per unit change in the explanatory variable.

###### Option 3
Exactly 80% of the variation in patient satisfaction scores can be explained by the number of nurses per shift — Confuses slope with R². This targets students who misidentify the slope as the coefficient of determination. The value 0.8 interpreted as 80% suggests the student converted the decimal to a percentage, a common error when mixing up regression output statistics.

###### Option 4
Patient satisfaction scores are 0.8 times the number of nurses working per shift — Misinterprets regression as proportional relationship. This targets students who treat the slope as a simple multiplier rather than understanding that regression models predict average change and include an intercept term. This reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of how linear regression models relationships.
