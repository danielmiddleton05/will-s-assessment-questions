##### Explanation
An upward-sloping trend line indicates a general pattern of increase over time. In a time series, the trend represents the change in the mean value $E(x)$ over the period. While individual data points fluctuate above and below the line due to quarterly variance or seasonal patterns, the positive slope of the linear trend line indicates a central tendency of growth over the three-year period.

##### Domain

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The company's revenue showed a downward trend over the period — A downward trend would be indicated by a line that slopes down from left to right (a negative slope). The chart shows a line sloping upward, indicating a positive relationship between time and revenue.

###### Option 3
The company's revenue showed a seasonal trend over the period — This distractor confuses the "trend" component with the "seasonal" component of a time series. While the blue data points may show recurring quarterly fluctuations, the trend line itself is a straight line that filters out these fluctuations to show the long-term direction of the data.

###### Option 4
The company's revenue showed no clear trend over the period — No clear trend occurs when the data points are scattered such that there is no discernible relationship with time, which would result in a horizontal trend line (slope of zero). The chart shows a distinct, positive linear slope.
