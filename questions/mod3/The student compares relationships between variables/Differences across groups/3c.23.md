#### Task Description

**Given:** A rehabilitation center studies the relationship between weekly hydrotherapy sessions and patient pain reduction scores at discharge for two groups. Higher pain reduction score values indicate greater reductions in pain. The correlation coefficient (r) is $0.81$ for the post-surgical patients group, and $-0.34$ for the chronic pain patients group. A hospital administrator proposes increasing hydrotherapy sessions for all patients to improve pain reduction scores.

Why is this proposal problematic based on the reported correlations?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
The correlation for post-surgical patients is too weak to justify increasing hydrotherapy sessions.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Both patient groups show a similar positive association between hydrotherapy sessions and pain reduction.


###### Option 3 Correct
More hydrotherapy sessions are associated with lower pain reduction scores for chronic pain patients.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Chronic pain patients simply require more hydrotherapy sessions than post-surgical patients to improve.

##### Explanation
The correlation coefficient for post-surgical patients (r = 0.81) indicates a strong positive relationship—more hydrotherapy sessions are associated with higher pain reduction scores. However, the correlation for chronic pain patients (r = −0.34) indicates a negative relationship—more hydrotherapy sessions are associated with lower pain reduction scores. Because the two groups have opposite directional relationships with the same variable (hydrotherapy sessions), a blanket policy of increasing hydrotherapy for all patients is problematic. The administrator's proposal ignores that the relationship between hydrotherapy sessions and pain reduction differs across groups, and applying it uniformly could be counterproductive for chronic pain patients.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The correlation for post-surgical patients is too weak to justify increasing hydrotherapy sessions — A correlation of r = 0.81 is a strong positive correlation, not a weak one. A student who is unfamiliar with how to interpret the magnitude of correlation coefficients might mistakenly consider any value less than 1.0 to be weak.

###### Option 2
Both patient groups show a similar positive association between hydrotherapy sessions and pain reduction — This is incorrect because the chronic pain group has a negative correlation (r = −0.34), not a positive one. A student who overlooks or misunderstands the negative sign may assume both groups show the same directional relationship.

###### Option 4
Chronic pain patients simply require more hydrotherapy sessions than post-surgical patients to improve — This misinterprets the negative correlation as meaning that chronic pain patients just have not yet received enough hydrotherapy to benefit. A negative correlation does not indicate a threshold effect; it indicates that higher values of one variable are associated with lower values of the other. This is a plausible misconception for a novice who confuses dosage with directionality.
