#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital researcher creates the following scatterplot examining the relationship between patient age (in years) and recovery time (in days) following knee replacement surgery. The scatterplot reveals an upward trend, but the data points are widely scattered around the trend line rather than closely clustered.

![Scatterplot of Patient Age vs. Recovery Time showing an upward trend line with data points widely scattered around it.](https://k3-production-bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/865a4f1d-0784-48f6-9ec2-1331f13bf5b0_optimized.jpg)

What does the wide scatter of data points around the trend line indicate about this relationship?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 2 Incorrect
Age and recovery time have a perfect positive correlation.


###### Option 3 Correct
Age alone does not fully explain the variation in recovery time.

Age and recovery time have no relationship.

Variability (spread around the pattern)

##### Explanation
When data points are widely scattered around a trend line, this indicates high variability in the relationship. While the upward trend suggests that age is associated with recovery time, the wide spread shows that age does not account for all the variation in recovery outcomes. Other factors—such as overall health, physical fitness, or post-surgical care—likely also influence recovery time. Option 2 is incorrect because "perfect correlation" would mean all points fall exactly on the trend line with no scatter. Option 4 is incorrect because an upward trend is present, indicating a relationship exists. Option 1 contradicts the upward trend described in the scenario.

##### Domain
Health

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Recovery time decreases as patient age increases — Misreads direction
This directly contradicts the "upward trend" stated in the given. Students selecting this option may have misread the scenario or do not understand that an upward trend in a scatterplot means both variables increase together.

###### Option 2
Age and recovery time have a perfect positive correlation — Confuses the existence of a trend with its strength
This targets students who believe that the presence of a trend line implies a perfect or deterministic relationship. They fail to understand that "scatter" represents the degree to which the relationship is *not* perfect and that a correlation is only "perfect" when there is zero spread around the line.

###### Option 4
Age and recovery time have no relationship — Confuses weak with absent
This targets students who conflate high variability with no relationship. The scenario explicitly states there is an upward trend, so a relationship exists. Students selecting this option fail to distinguish between the presence of a pattern (direction) and the precision of predictions (strength/variability).
