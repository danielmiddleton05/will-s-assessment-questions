#### Task Description

**Given:** A regional sales manager reviews a regression analysis comparing monthly advertising budget to sales revenue for two product lines. Both analyses reveal a positive linear trend. The table below summarizes the residual statistics from each regression model.

| Product Line | Residual Variance | Residual Standard Deviation |
|---|---|---|
| A | 2,500 | $50 |
| B | 40,000 | $200 |

What does the difference in residual standard deviation and variance reveal about using advertising budget to predict sales revenue for these product lines?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Incorrect
Product Line B's larger residual variance suggests that advertising budget has a stronger overall influence on revenue for that product line.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The difference in residual variance between product lines reflects the natural variability in each market, not a difference in how advertising budget predicts revenue.


###### Option 3 Correct
Product Line A's smaller residual standard deviation indicates that revenue predictions based on advertising budget will be more consistent for this product line.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Product Line B's higher residual variance indicates that its regression model accounts for a wider range of revenue outcomes, making its predictions more broadly applicable.

##### Explanation
Residual standard deviation and variance measure how far individual data points fall from the regression line. A smaller residual standard deviation (Product Line A at $50 vs. Product Line B at $200) means the actual revenue values cluster more tightly around the predicted values, producing more consistent predictions. Product Line B's residual standard deviation is four times larger, meaning actual revenues deviate much more from the predicted trend, so forecasts for that product line carry substantially more uncertainty. Both the variance and standard deviation statistics confirm the same conclusion: less spread around the regression line translates to more dependable predictions.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Product Line B's larger residual variance suggests that advertising budget has a stronger overall influence on revenue for that product line. — Confuses residual variance with predictor influence

This distractor conflates residual variance with the strength or magnitude of the advertising-revenue relationship. Larger residual variance means observed revenues deviate more widely from predicted values, indicating weaker model fit—not a stronger influence of advertising on revenue. Students selecting this may associate larger numerical values in the residual statistics with a more impactful relationship, confusing prediction error with effect size.

###### Option 2
The difference in residual variance between product lines reflects the natural variability in each market, not a meaningful difference in how reliably advertising budget predicts revenue. — Misattributes residual differences to market context

This distractor introduces a plausible-sounding alternative explanation by attributing the residual gap to inherent market differences rather than model fit. Residual standard deviation is expressed in comparable dollar units for both models, and the fourfold difference ($50 vs. $200) directly reflects how closely predictions match actual outcomes—not simply the character of each market. Students selecting this may look for an external explanation for the residual gap rather than recognizing that residual spread is itself the measure of prediction reliability.

###### Option 4
Product Line B's higher residual variance indicates that its regression model accounts for a wider range of revenue outcomes, making its predictions more broadly applicable. — Misinterprets residual spread as model coverage

This distractor appeals to the idea that a model with higher residual variance is somehow more comprehensive or generalizable. In reality, higher residual variance means observed values scatter more widely around predicted values, indicating greater prediction error. Students may confuse the concept of a model's range of applicability with residual spread, which measures how closely predictions match actual outcomes rather than how broadly the model can be used.
