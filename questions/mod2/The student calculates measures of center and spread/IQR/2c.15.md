#### Task Description

A financial analyst determines that the interquartile range (IQR) of monthly profits for a company's regional offices is \$32,000. The analyst also notes that exactly half of the offices had profits below \$67,000.

Which of the following sets of values for the first quartile (Q1) and third quartile (Q3) is consistent with this data?

###### Option 3 Correct
Q1 = \$52,000; Q3 = \$84,000


###### Option 4 Incorrect
Q1 = \$80,000; Q3 = \$112,000

##### Explanation
The interquartile range (IQR) is the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1): $IQR = Q3 - Q1$. Given an IQR of \$32,000, we must find the pair where $Q3 - Q1 = \$32,000$. Additionally, the problem states half the offices are below \$67,000, meaning the median is \$67,000. In a valid distribution, Q1 must be less than the median, and Q3 must be greater than the median. All four options produce $Q3 - Q1 = \$32,000$, so the IQR alone does not distinguish the correct answer. However, only Option 3 also satisfies the median constraint: $\$84,000 - \$52,000 = \$32,000$, and the median (\$67,000) correctly falls between Q1 (\$52,000) and Q3 (\$84,000).

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The student finds a pair with the correct difference of \$32,000 (\$57,000 − \$25,000 = \$32,000) but fails to verify that the median (\$67,000) falls between Q1 and Q3. Here, Q3 = \$57,000 is below the median, placing both quartiles in the lower half of the distribution. This error reflects a misconception that the IQR describes the spread of the lower portion of the data rather than the middle 50%.

###### Option 2
The student finds a pair with the correct difference of \$32,000 (\$102,000 − \$70,000 = \$32,000) but places Q1 = \$70,000 above the median (\$67,000), putting both quartiles in the upper half of the distribution. This error reflects a misconception that the IQR describes the spread of the upper portion of the data, treating the median as a lower boundary rather than a value that falls between Q1 and Q3.

###### Option 4
The student finds a pair with the correct difference of \$32,000 (\$112,000 − \$80,000 = \$32,000) but places both Q1 and Q3 well above the median (\$67,000). This error occurs when the student focuses exclusively on satisfying the IQR computation without considering the median's position relative to the quartiles, disregarding the constraint that Q1 < median < Q3.
