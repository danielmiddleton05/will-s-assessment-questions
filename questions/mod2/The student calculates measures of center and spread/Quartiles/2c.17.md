##### Explanation
For a dataset of 15 values arranged in order, the median (Q2) is at the 8th position, calculated as $(15+1)/2 = 8$. The median value is 59. This divides the data into a lower half of 7 values (positions 1–7) and an upper half of 7 values (positions 9–15). The first quartile (Q1) is the median of the lower half: 15, 22, 28, **34**, 42, 49, 53. For 7 values, the median is at position $(7+1)/2 = 4$. The 4th value is **34**, so Q1 = 34.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
28 — Uses the 3rd position instead of the 4th.

The student calculates the Q1 position as $n/4 = 15/4 = 3.75$ and rounds down to position 3, selecting 28. This error comes from applying a locator formula ($n/4$) and rounding down, instead of correctly finding the median of the lower half, which has 7 values and a median at the 4th position.

###### Option 2
31 — Incorrectly averages the 3rd and 4th values.

The student calculates $n/4 = 15/4 = 3.75$, interprets this as falling between the 3rd and 4th positions, and averages those values: $(28 + 34)/2 = 31$. This error applies the even-count median rule (averaging two middle values) to the quartile location, even though the lower half has 7 values (an odd count) with a single middle value at the 4th position.

###### Option 4
37 — Divides the range into four equal parts instead of finding the median of the lower half.

The student calculates Q1 as the value one-quarter of the way from the minimum to the maximum: $15 + (103 - 15)/4 = 15 + 22 = 37$. This error stems from confusing quartiles of a dataset (which divide the *data points* into four equal groups) with quartiles of the *range* (which divide the interval between the minimum and maximum into four equal parts).
