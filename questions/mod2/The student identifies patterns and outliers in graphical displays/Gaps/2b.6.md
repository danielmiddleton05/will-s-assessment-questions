Gaps

##### Explanation
A gap in a distribution is an interval where there are few or no observations, creating an empty or nearly empty space in the graphical display. The description indicates very few patients in the 40-50 range, which creates a gap between the two clusters of data.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
An outlier — Confuses gap with individual extreme value
An outlier is an individual data point that falls far from the rest of the data, not an empty range. Students might confuse the concept of "unusual" with gaps, but outliers are specific values, not intervals.

###### Option 2
The center — Confuses position with frequency
While 40-50 might appear to be "in the middle" of the age range numerically, center refers to where data values concentrate, not the midpoint of the range. A gap cannot be the center of a distribution.

###### Option 4
A peak — Opposite feature
A peak (or mode) is where data clusters together with high frequency, the opposite of a gap. Students who reverse the concepts might select this option.
