##### Explanation
The tallest bar in a histogram represents the interval with the highest frequency—this is the modal class of the distribution. The modal class indicates the most commonly occurring range of values. The maximum value, total frequency, and median cannot be determined simply by identifying the tallest bar.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
The maximum value of the distribution — Confuses frequency with magnitude
The maximum value refers to the highest data value along the x-axis (the rightmost bar), not the tallest bar. Students often confuse the "height" of the bar with the "value" of the data.

###### Option 2
The total frequency of the distribution — Confuses interval frequency with total sample
The total frequency is the sum of the heights of all bars in the histogram. The tallest bar represents only the frequency for that specific interval, not the total number of data points.

###### Option 4
The median of the distribution — Confuses modal class with median
The median is the middle value when data is ordered and requires knowing the total count to locate. It is not necessarily in the tallest bar, especially in skewed distributions.
