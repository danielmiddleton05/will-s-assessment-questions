#### Task Description

A hospital researcher collects patient data including patient age in years, blood type, length of hospital stay in days, and body temperature in degrees Fahrenheit.

For which variable would calculating an average (mean) be inappropriate?

#### Internal Notes

##### Competency
The student interprets real-world data using graphical displays and descriptive statistics.

##### Evidence Statement
The student identifies types of data.

##### Aligned Content
Categorical vs numerical

##### Domain
Healthcare

##### Explanation
Blood type is a categorical variable representing distinct classifications without numerical meaning. Calculating an average of blood types is meaningless because the categories have no inherent numerical order or value that can be mathematically combined. In contrast, patient age, length of stay, and body temperature are all numerical variables for which calculating a mean provides meaningful information about the typical value.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Patient age in years — Assumes discrete numerical data cannot be averaged
Students may incorrectly select this if they confuse discrete numerical data (whole numbers representing age) with categorical data. Age in years is numerical and can be meaningfully averaged to determine the typical age of patients in the dataset.

###### Option 3
Length of hospital stay in days — Confuses count data with categorical
This targets students who may think that because days are counted in whole numbers, they represent categories rather than quantities. Length of stay is discrete numerical data representing a count that can be meaningfully summarized with a mean.

###### Option 4
Body temperature in degrees Fahrenheit — Misunderstands continuous measurement data
Students selecting this may not recognize that temperature is a continuous numerical measurement. This targets those who might confuse measured values expressed in specific units with categorical classifications.
