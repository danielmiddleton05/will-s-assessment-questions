**Given:** A retail company tracks monthly customer satisfaction scores (0-100) for two store locations. The box plots below summarize 24 months of data.

Which conclusion about customer satisfaction is supported by these box plots?

#### Quiz Options

Store A typically has higher satisfaction scores than Store B.


###### Option 2 Incorrect
Store B's satisfaction scores are more consistent than Store A's.


###### Option 3 Correct
Store A's satisfaction scores are more consistent than Store B's.


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The stores have different typical satisfaction levels but similar variability.

##### Explanation
Both stores have the same median satisfaction score of 75, indicating similar centers. However, Store A has an interquartile range of 10 points (80 - 70) and a total range of 30 points (90 - 60), while Store B has an interquartile range of 20 points (85 - 65) and a total range of 50 points (100 - 50). Since Store A has smaller spread in both measures, its scores are more consistent (less variable) than Store B's scores.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
This option incorrectly compares the centers. Both box plots clearly show medians at 75, indicating the typical satisfaction score is the same for both stores. Students may misread the box plots or confuse spread differences with center differences.

###### Option 2
This option reverses the consistency comparison. Consistency relates to spread—smaller spread means more consistency. Store B has a larger IQR (20 vs. 10) and larger range (50 vs. 30), making it less consistent, not more. Students may confuse which store has greater variability or misunderstand the relationship between spread and consistency.

###### Option 4
This option gets both comparisons backward. The stores have similar typical levels (both medians at 75) but different variability (Store A's range is 30, Store B's range is 50). Students may correctly notice differences exist but misidentify which measure differs and which is similar.
