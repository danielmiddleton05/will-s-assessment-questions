##### Explanation
The IQR is calculated as $\text{IQR} = Q3 - Q1$. For Clinic A: $\text{IQR} = 55 - 35 = 20$ years. For Clinic B: $\text{IQR} = 60 - 40 = 20$ years. Both clinics have equal IQRs of 20 years, meaning the middle 50% of patient ages span the same range at both clinics. Despite having different quartile values, the spread of the middle half of the data is identical for both clinics.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Students might assume that because Clinic A's quartile values differ from Clinic B's, the IQRs must also differ, without performing the actual calculation to verify that the differences between Q3 and Q1 are the same for both clinics.

###### Option 2
Students might think that Clinic B's higher Q1 and Q3 values (40 and 60) compared to Clinic A's values (35 and 55) translate to a larger IQR, not recognizing that what matters is the difference between the quartiles rather than their absolute values.

###### Option 4
Students might misunderstand the components displayed in box plots, not recognizing that box plots explicitly show the quartiles (Q1 and Q3) needed to calculate IQR. This tests whether students understand what information box plots provide and how it relates to measures of spread.
