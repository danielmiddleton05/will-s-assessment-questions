##### Explanation
The distribution is skewed left (negatively skewed) because the tail extends toward lower values (lower exam scores). In the box plot, this is evident from the median being positioned closer to the third quartile (upper edge of the box) and the lower whisker extending much further than the upper whisker. When most data clusters at higher values with a long tail stretching toward lower values, the distribution is left-skewed.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Symmetric — Ignores asymmetry in the box plot
Symmetric distributions show equal whisker lengths and a centered median within the box. Students may select this if they focus only on the central box without examining the relative whisker lengths and median position.

###### Option 2
Skewed right — Reverses skew direction
Right-skewed distributions have tails extending toward higher values, shown by a longer upper whisker and median closer to Q1. Students may confuse which direction the tail extends or misremember the terminology for skewness direction.

###### Option 3
Bimodal — Misidentifies modality
Bimodal distributions have two distinct peaks. This option targets students who are confused about which features of a distribution are visible in a box plot, as box plots generally obscure modality, or those who misinterpret the outliers as representing a second cluster of data.
