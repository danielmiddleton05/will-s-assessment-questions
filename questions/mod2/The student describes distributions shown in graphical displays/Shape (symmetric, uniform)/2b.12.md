Shape (symmetric, uniform)

##### Explanation
A uniform distribution has approximately equal frequencies across all intervals, meaning each value or range occurs with similar frequency. The equal-height bars across all intervals indicate a uniform distribution.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
Symmetric and bell-shaped — Confuses uniform with normal distribution

A bell-shaped (normal) distribution has frequencies that peak in the middle and gradually taper toward both ends, creating a characteristic bell curve. Equal bar heights indicate uniform distribution, not the peaked pattern of a bell curve.

###### Option 2
Right-skewed — Ignores equal distribution pattern

A right-skewed distribution shows most frequencies concentrated on the left with a long tail extending to the right, resulting in decreasing bar heights. The equal heights contradict any form of skewness.

###### Option 4
Left-skewed — Misinterprets minor variation as skewness

A left-skewed distribution shows most frequencies concentrated on the right with a long tail extending to the left, resulting in increasing bar heights. The approximately equal heights across all intervals indicate a uniform pattern, not a skewed one.
