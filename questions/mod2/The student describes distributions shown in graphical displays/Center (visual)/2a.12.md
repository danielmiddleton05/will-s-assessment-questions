##### Explanation
The visual center of a distribution is where data appears most concentrated or balanced. Since the dots cluster densely around 145 milliseconds, this represents the center of the distribution and the typical value. The visual center should not be confused with the endpoints of the data range.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Selects the minimum region — Students may incorrectly identify the lower boundary of the data range rather than where the data clusters most densely. This represents a fundamental misunderstanding of visual center versus data range.

###### Option 2
Estimates visual center too low — Students may underestimate the center by focusing disproportionately on the lower portion of the distribution, resulting in a value below the actual clustering point.

###### Option 4
Selects the maximum region — Students may incorrectly identify the upper boundary of the data range rather than the visual center where clustering occurs. This shows confusion between range endpoints and measures of center.
