##### Explanation
To solve for $h$, first subtract the constant from both sides of the equation: $450 - 90 = 30h$, which gives $360 = 30h$. Then divide both sides by the coefficient of 30: $h = 360 \div 30 = 12$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Divides total cost by the constant term instead of the coefficient: $450 \div 90 = 5$. This error targets students who confuse the roles of the coefficient and constant in a linear equation. They recognize division is needed but select the wrong value to divide by, showing a misunderstanding of equation structure.

###### Option 3
Ignores the constant term entirely: $450 \div 30 = 15$. This targets students who recognize the relationship between the total and the hourly rate but fail to account for the fixed cost that must be subtracted first. They treat the equation as $T = 30h$, overlooking the $+90$ term.

###### Option 4
Adds the constant instead of subtracting it: $(450 + 90) \div 30 = 540 \div 30 = 18$. This error targets students who recognize that they must address the constant term before dividing but apply the inverse operation. Instead of subtracting 90 to undo the addition in the original formula, they add it, demonstrating confusion about inverse operations when isolating a variable.
