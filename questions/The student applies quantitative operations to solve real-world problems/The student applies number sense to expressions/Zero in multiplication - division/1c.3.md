#### Task Description

**Given:** A retail company calculates daily revenue using: $R = N \times V$, where $R$ is the daily revenue, $N$ is the number of transactions and $V$ is the average transaction value. On a day the store was unexpectedly closed, the store recorded 0 transactions. The average transaction value from the previous operating day was $85.

What was the store's daily revenue on this day?

##### Explanation
The daily revenue is $0 \times 85 = \$0$. Zero multiplied by any number equals zero. When one factor in a multiplication is zero, the product is always zero, regardless of the other factor's value. This question assesses understanding of the zero property of multiplication in a practical business revenue context, requiring students to apply the multiplication formula even when one input is zero.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
\$1 — Confuses the zero property of multiplication with the zero exponent rule. Students may recall that "anything raised to the power of zero equals one" ($85^0 = 1$) and mistakenly apply this exponent rule to a multiplication context, thinking that zero appearing in an operation always produces one. This reflects a conflation of distinct mathematical properties involving zero.

###### Option 3
\$85 — Misapplies identity properties. Students may incorrectly believe the average transaction value alone represents the revenue, or confuse the zero property with the multiplicative identity property where multiplying by 1 (not 0) preserves the value. Some students may also simply default to the only concrete number given in the problem.

###### Option 4
Undefined — Confuses multiplication by zero with division by zero. Students may remember that zero creates "undefined" situations in certain operations and incorrectly apply this concept to multiplication rather than recognizing it only applies when dividing by zero.
