##### Explanation
The contamination rate is $0 \div 2,400 = 0$. Zero divided by any non-zero number equals zero. The result would only be undefined if dividing by zero, which is not the case here. This question assesses understanding of division involving zero in a practical lab quality control context.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
1 — Misapplies identity properties. Students may incorrectly believe that any division involving zero yields a special result like $1$, or confuse this with the multiplicative identity property (any number divided by itself equals $1$).

###### Option 3
∞ (Infinity) — Misinterprets the rate by reversing the division (computing $2,400 \div 0$) and incorrectly believes division by zero results in “infinity” rather than being undefined.

###### Option 4
Undefined — Confuses zero in numerator vs. denominator. This targets students who conflate $0 \div n = 0$ (valid, equals zero) with $n \div 0 = \text{undefined}$ (division by zero). Students may remember that "zero and division cause problems" without understanding which position creates the issue.
