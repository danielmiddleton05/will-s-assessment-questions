**Given:** A community health center is preparing emergency supply kits. The center has 60 sterile bandages and 48 pairs of medical gloves. Each kit must contain the same number of bandages and the same number of gloves, with no supplies left over.

What is the greatest number of kits that can be prepared?

##### Explanation
To find the greatest number of kits that can be prepared with equal distribution and no supplies remaining, determine the greatest common factor (GCF) of 60 and 48. Find the factors: Factors of 60 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60. Factors of 48 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48. The greatest common factor is 12. Therefore, 12 kits can be prepared, with each kit containing 5 bandages (60 ÷ 12 = 5) and 4 pairs of gloves (48 ÷ 12 = 4).

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
This option targets students who identify a common factor of both 60 and 48, but fail to find the greatest common factor. While 4 does divide both numbers evenly, it is not the largest number that does so. This represents an incomplete factorization process.

###### Option 3
This option targets students who incorrectly add the two quantities together. Computing $60 + 48 = 108$ is a common error when students confuse the problem structure with a simple addition scenario rather than recognizing this as a factorization problem requiring division.

###### Option 4
This option targets students who find the least common multiple (LCM) instead of the greatest common factor (GCF). The LCM of 60 and 48 is 240, which answers "What is the smallest number divisible by both?" rather than "What is the largest number that divides both?" This represents confusion between two related but distinct concepts in number theory.
