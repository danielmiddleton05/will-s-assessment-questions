##### Explanation
To convert $0.625$ to a fraction in lowest terms, first express it as a fraction: $0.625 = \frac{625}{1000}$ (since the decimal has three places). Next, find the greatest common factor (GCF) of $625$ and $1000$, which is $125$. Dividing both numerator and denominator by $125$ yields: $625 \div 125 = 5$ and $1000 \div 125 = 8$, giving the fraction $\frac{5}{8}$ in lowest terms.

##### Distractors

###### Option 1
$\frac{3}{8}$ — Converting the complement
Student subtracts $0.625$ from $1$, obtaining $0.375$, and then converts $0.375$ to a fraction ($\frac{375}{1000} = \frac{3}{8}$). This error comes from finding the complement (the portion of schools that did *not* meet targets) rather than converting the given decimal directly. The result $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$ does not equal $0.625$.

###### Option 2
$\frac{3}{5}$ — Rounding before conversion
Student rounds the decimal $0.625$ to $0.6$ before converting to a fraction, then correctly expresses $0.6$ as $\frac{3}{5}$ in lowest terms. This error stems from approximating first rather than converting the exact decimal value, resulting in $\frac{3}{5} = 0.6$ instead of the correct $\frac{5}{8} = 0.625$.

###### Option 4
$\frac{5}{6}$ — Arithmetic error when simplifying
Student correctly writes $\frac{625}{1000}$ and identifies the GCF as $125$. They correctly divide the numerator ($625 \div 125 = 5$) but make an arithmetic error dividing the denominator, calculating $1000 \div 125 = 6$ instead of $8$. This produces $\frac{5}{6} \approx 0.833$, which does not equal $0.625$. The error reflects a computational mistake during simplification rather than a misunderstanding of the conversion process itself.
