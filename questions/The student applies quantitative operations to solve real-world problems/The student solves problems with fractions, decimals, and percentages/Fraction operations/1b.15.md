#### Task Description

A business owner owns $\frac{5}{6}$ of a company's total shares. To raise capital, the owner sells $\frac{2}{9}$ of the total company shares to a private investor.

What fraction of the total company shares does the owner retain?

##### Explanation
To find the remaining fraction, subtract $\frac{2}{9}$ from $\frac{5}{6}$. First, find the least common denominator of 6 and 9, which is 18. Convert the fractions: $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{15}{18}$ and $\frac{2}{9} = \frac{4}{18}$. Then subtract: $\frac{15}{18} - \frac{4}{18} = \frac{11}{18}$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
Fails to convert numerators and adds — Student finds the correct common denominator (18) but fails to convert the numerators and incorrectly adds the original numerators ($5 + 2 = 7$) instead of subtracting the converted values.

###### Option 2
Uses numerator of minuend as subtrahend — Student correctly converts the first fraction to $\frac{15}{18}$ but incorrectly subtracts the original numerator of the first fraction ($5$) instead of the converted numerator of the second fraction ($4$), resulting in $\frac{15}{18} - \frac{5}{18} = \frac{10}{18} = \frac{5}{9}$.

###### Option 4
Fails to multiply numerator when converting — Student correctly converts the first fraction to $\frac{15}{18}$ but fails to multiply the numerator of the second fraction by the scaling factor, leaving it as $\frac{2}{18}$. The result is $\frac{15}{18} - \frac{2}{18} = \frac{13}{18}$.
