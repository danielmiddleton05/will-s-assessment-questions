#### Task Description

**Given:** A hospital emergency department has 3 physicians who can treat 42 patients during a 12-hour shift. The department manager sets up the following proportion to plan staffing:

$$\frac{3}{42} = \frac{x}{98}$$

What does solving for $x$ in this proportion determine?

#### Quiz Options

###### Option 1 Correct
The number of physicians needed to treat 98 patients in a 12-hour shift


###### Option 2 Incorrect
The number of patients that 98 physicians can treat in a 12-hour shift


###### Option 3 Incorrect
The number of hours needed for 3 physicians to treat 98 patients


###### Option 4 Incorrect
The number of shifts required for 3 physicians to treat 98 patients

##### Explanation
The proportion $\frac{3}{42} = \frac{x}{98}$ maintains the relationship of physicians to patients. The left side shows 3 physicians for 42 patients, and the right side represents $x$ physicians for 98 patients. Since both ratios compare physicians (numerator) to patients (denominator), solving for $x$ determines how many physicians are needed to maintain the same physician-to-patient ratio when treating 98 patients. The 12-hour shift timeframe remains constant across both scenarios and doesn't change the proportional relationship.

##### Distractors

###### Option 2
Reverses the interpretation by treating $x$ as if it represents patients rather than physicians, and interprets 98 as the number of physicians. This reflects a misunderstanding of which variable the proportion is solving for and what each number in the proportion represents. If 98 were physicians and we wanted patients, the proportion would be $\frac{3}{42} = \frac{98}{x}$ or $\frac{42}{3} = \frac{x}{98}$.

###### Option 3
Introduces time (hours) as the variable being solved for, even though time is held constant in the scenario and doesn't appear as a variable in the proportion. This distractor appeals to students who recognize that both staffing and time affect patient care capacity but fail to identify that the proportion specifically compares physicians to patients, not time to patients.

###### Option 4
Confuses the concept of shifts with the proportional relationship being established. While shifts are mentioned in the context, they are not what the proportion calculates. This distractor appeals to students who notice the 12-hour shift mentioned in the scenario and incorrectly assume the proportion must be solving for something related to shifts rather than recognizing it maintains a consistent physician-to-patient ratio.
