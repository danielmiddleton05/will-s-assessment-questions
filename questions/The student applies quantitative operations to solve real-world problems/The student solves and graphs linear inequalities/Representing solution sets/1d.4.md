**Given:** A small business earns 18 US dollars in revenue per unit sold and has fixed monthly costs of 540 US dollars. Monthly profit is calculated as total revenue minus fixed costs.

##### Explanation
Monthly profit is modeled as $P = 18x - 540$, where $x$ is the number of units sold. For a positive profit, the inequality is $18x - 540 > 0$. Solving: $18x > 540$, so $x > 30$. Because the inequality is strict (profit must be greater than zero, not equal to zero), the endpoint 30 is excluded, which is shown with a parenthesis. The solution set extending to the right without bound is represented as $(30, \infty)$.

##### Distractors
###### Option 1
$[30, \infty)$ — This distractor is intentionally designed to assess whether the student distinguishes between strict and inclusive inequality notation, a core skill within the evidence statement. At $x = 30$, profit equals $18(30) - 540 = 0$, which is not a positive profit. A student choosing this option solved the algebra correctly but misinterpreted the problem condition: the prompt requires strictly positive profit ($P > 0$, yielding $x > 30$ with an excluded endpoint), not non-negative profit ($P \geq 0$, which would include 30). Confusing "greater than" with "greater than or equal to" when translating word problems into interval notation is a well-documented common error that this distractor deliberately targets.

###### Option 3
$[540, \infty)$ — This combines two errors: using the fixed cost value of 540 directly as the boundary rather than dividing both sides by 18, and including the endpoint with a square bracket rather than excluding it with a parenthesis.

###### Option 4
$(540, \infty)$ — This uses the fixed cost value of 540 directly as the boundary rather than solving the inequality. A student choosing this option likely recognized that the number of units must exceed some threshold but skipped the algebraic step of dividing both sides by 18.
